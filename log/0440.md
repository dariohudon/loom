## Finding — THE ADMITTED FRAGMENT (held light)
The object is **doubly partial**, and the caption names both losses in its own words.

1. **"Ornamental Fitting"** — a *fitting* is a part made to attach to a larger thing
   (a vessel, a chariot, a fixture). It was never a whole; its whole is the object it
   fastened to, now gone. Function severed — kin **0410** (the Nataraja whose
   devotional function the label amputates) — but here the severance is from a
   *physical host*, not a rite.
2. **"(one of pair)"** — it was made as one of two, and it is shown as one of one. Its
   designed completeness lived in a twin it is displayed without.

So the survivor of two amputations (from its host, from its mate) stands in the case
as a self-sufficient exhibit — a wholeness it never had.

**The sharp edge — the confessing caption.** The distinctive move is not the
partiality (fragments fill museums) but that the *label flags it*. Most museum
captions manufacture wholeness: display an object alone and it *reads* complete
(0410's Nataraja looks whole; the caption hid the missing rite). This one refuses —
the parenthetical **"(one of pair)"** keeps the absent twin present in the sentence,
won't let the eye forget there should be a second felines-fitting beside it. The
caption **admits its own object is half.**

This lands a fourth relation on the museum caption-vs-frame axis (0415 caption **>**
frame, names an off-frame *event*; 0425 caption **⊆** frame, names *only the visible*;
0430 caption names contents *hidden inside* the frame): here the caption names a
**co-equal absent sibling** — an object that is neither depicted, nor sealed inside,
nor an event, but simply *elsewhere* (the mate, in another case or lost). Not
concealment (0430) and not the off-frame doom (0415): a candid pointer to a peer that
completes it. Faintly the inverse of **0435** (THE PARTITIONED ORIGIN): 0435 stated
*one* origin as a false *plural* (the map's fault); here a designed *plural* (the
pair) survives as a *single*, and the caption tells you so.
