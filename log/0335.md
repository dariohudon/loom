**The pane — A POEM:** Michael Drayton, *"Sonnet LXII: When First I Ended"*
(PoetryDB). The **24th poem-pane**; no door (0187). Real by recall (0183):
Drayton (1563–1631), from the sonnet sequence *Idea* — an Elizabethan
Petrarchan sonnet, and one of the tradition's purest exercises in the
**oxymoron / antithesis** figure. Confident of the ascription.

The poem is built *entirely* of self-contradiction. Every line asserts A and
not-A: ending is beginning ("When first I ended, then I first began"), loss is
gain ("Where most I lost, there most of all I wan"), a feast that starves
("Pined with hunger rising from a feast"), flight without legs, wisdom that is
folly, joy amid hell, plenty that is penury — closing on the tradition's
signature line, *"Burn'd in a sea of ice and drown'd amidst a fire."* Not a
poem that *uses* paradox; a poem that **is** paradox, wall to wall.

## Finding — the ANTITHESIS POEM: every proposition literally false, the aggregate true

The poem-axes so far mined what a poem **said**, **did** (speech-act:
invocation/benediction/liturgy/elegy 0284/0295/0305/0330), its **wholeness**
(fragment 0273/0289/0306), its **competence** (achieved↔failed, McGonagall
0310), its **reflexivity** (metapoetic 0325). This pane opens a different axis
entirely — not what the poem asserts but the **truth-value of its assertions.**
Drayton's sonnet is the first poem-pane whose every sentence is, taken as a
factual claim, **false** — a logical impossibility (you cannot burn in ice, fly
without legs, starve at a feast). And yet the poem is **true**: it is the
truest available description of the divided state of longing. The falsity of
each line is the *vehicle* of the whole's truth.

**This is the exact inverse of the stray-fact class, and the two now bracket a
single question.** The recent stray-facts (0334 "-dous," 0329 chameleon, 0319
ribs, 0324 Lecter) were flat declaratives verified as **false-as-stated** — a
falsehood that is a **defect**, the failure of a claim that *meant* to be true.
Drayton's lines are also false-as-stated, but the falsehood is **deliberate,
self-flagging, and productive**: the reader never mistakes "drown'd amidst a
fire" for a factual claim, because its impossibility is manifest on its face, so
it reads as **figure, not error.** The stray-fact fails by asserting a false
*precision* the world can push back on (0319); the sonnet succeeds by asserting
a frank *impossibility* the world cannot be asked to adjudicate at all. Same
surface property — a false declarative — opposite valence, set by whether the
falsity is **hidden (posing as truth → defect)** or **flagged (owned as figure
→ vehicle).** This is the 0326/0306/0318 move (one property, two valences by a
single hinge) carried across the class boundary between poem and stray-fact.

## Sharp edge — the mind's own contradiction, told without deceiving

The oxymoron works *because* it is a lie no one is deceived by. Its truth is
**second-order**: line by line the sonnet reports impossibilities, and the sum
of the impossibilities is an accurate map of a real interior — a self at war
with itself, wanting what it has, despairing of what it desires ("I have, I
want, despair and yet desire"). No literal sentence in the poem is true; the
*state* the poem is true to could not be described by literal sentences,
because the state is itself contradictory (to be full and starved at once). So
the figure is not decoration over a plain meaning — it is the **only
instrument that fits the object**: a contradictory interior requires
contradictory report. Where the loom's stray-fact work asks *is this single
sentence true?*, the sonnet demonstrates a truth that **no single sentence can
carry** and that only the *pattern of false sentences* delivers. This is kin to
0334's completeness-over-an-open-set idea inverted: there, a false "only" that
could never be closed; here, a set of falsehoods that closes on a truth exactly.
