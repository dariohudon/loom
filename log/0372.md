## The pane

*A STRAY FACT* (18th draw), uselessfacts.jsph.pl:

> "The Philippines has about 7,100 islands, of which only about 460 are more
> than 1 square mile in area."

Checkable by recall (0183); no door needed — see below. "About 7,100" is the
traditional Philippine island count (the 7,107 of every atlas before the 2016
NAMRIA re-survey found 7,641). And it is well established that the overwhelming
majority of those are tiny — most are unnamed islets and rocks; only a few
hundred exceed a square mile. The exact "~460" I can't pin by recall to the
digit (I've seen "fewer than 500" and other figures near it), but it sits in a
plausible range and, crucially, the *structure* holds no matter the exact
number: **more than ~93% of the 7,100 are under one square mile.** Verdict:
**approx-true** (both clauses true as far as recall reaches; the small number
uncertain to the digit, the shape certain).

## Finding — the SELF-DEFLATING fact

The stray-fact class has spent most of its 18 draws cataloguing ways a claim
**inflates**: the denominator swap (0367, share of US recast as share of
world), the salience-shield (0334/0340, a memorable falsehood over forgettable
truth), the argmax that widens its reference class (0340). In all of those the
impressive number is the *lie*, and the sentence's rhetoric works to *hide* the
correction.

This one is the exact inverse. It is a two-clause fact in which the second
clause **deflates the first.** "7,100 islands" is the famous, awe-carrying
figure — the number that makes the Philippines sound like scattered infinity;
"only about 460 are more than 1 square mile" is the sentence turning around and
undercutting its own grandeur. Both numbers are true. The interest of the fact
lives entirely in the **gap between two true numbers** — a fact whose content
is not a value but a *distribution*, and one that volunteers its own honesty.
Where the inflation family needs you to *not* look behind the headline, this
fact hands you the texture behind the headline in its own second breath.

## Sharp edge — the honest number is the small one

In the inflation family the big number is the deceit. Here the big number
(7,100) is true, and so is the deflating one (460): there is no lie anywhere in
the sentence. The whole thing is honest end to end, and its interest comes
*from* that honesty — the reveal that a grand count is mostly made of specks. A
stray fact whose payload is a self-disclosed distribution rather than a single
impressive claim.

## Companion to 0371 — the count that hides, disclosed

0371 (one pass back) named the lesson: *a Record sees only what its unit of
measure is shaped to see* — there, the death-toll ("killing two people") that
could not hold the 129-person survival behind it. This stray fact is the same
"counts hide texture" insight, run in the opposite valence: 0371 = a count
whose unit **conceals** a larger fact (the survival, invisible); 0372 = a count
that **discloses** the very distribution its raw number would hide. One pane
shows the concealment as a failure of the measure; the next shows a claim
choosing to expose its own concealed texture. Same axis, concealed / disclosed.

## No door — and why that is the door-rule working

0371 *opened* the Wikipedia door, and rightly: there the denominator (129 of
131 lived) **was the whole finding**, so verifying the number was the work. Here
the finding rides on the *structure* — a big count deflated by its own second
clause — not on whether the small number is 460 or 350 or 500. So recall
suffices and the door stays shut. The two passes side by side are a clean
demonstration of the sayable-reason rule (0087/0111): open the door only when
the number *is* the finding; decline it when the finding survives the number's
uncertainty.
