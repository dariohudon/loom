## Verdict — approx-true, but endpoint-contingent

The number is suspiciously exact and it nearly matches a real, well-known figure:
the **Empire State Building Run-Up** climbs to the **86th-floor observatory**, a
course cited at **1,576 steps** (recall, unsworn). "1,575" sits one step off that —
plausibly the same count, transcribed loose. So the figure isn't invented; it's the
*race* number.

But "the **top** of the building" is not a fixed point. The ESB has at least three
candidate summits: the **86th-floor** main observatory (where the Run-Up ends, ~1,576
steps), the **102nd-floor** observatory higher up (a materially larger count, ~1,860
by common citation), and the **spire/antenna** above that (not walkable). The stated
number is true for *one* reading of "top" — the observatory the race treats as the
finish — and false for the architectural summit. The precision of "1,575" masks the
slack in "top."

## Finding — THE UNDEFINED SUMMIT (held light, no coin)

A precise measurement stated over an **under-defined endpoint.** Its truth turns not
on the count (which is right, for a destination) but on *where you decide the building
stops* — a definitional word left unfixed in the sentence.

This is the **index-contingent** family run one more axis. The recent members turned
on a *missing indexing word*:
- **0407** (DATELESS SNAPSHOT) — a missing **time**-word (true then / false now).
- **0437** (JURISDICTIONAL TRUTH) — a missing **space**-word (true within the city
  limit / a McDonald's just over the line).
- **0442** — a missing **definition-of-the-terminus** word: not *when* or *where* the
  count is taken but *which point counts as the end of the thing being measured.*

0407's index was temporal, 0437's was a fixed administrative boundary elsewhere in
space; this one is *internal to the object* — the ambiguity isn't a line drawn on a
map but the question of what the object's own "top" is (roof? higher deck? spire?).
The missing word decides the truth, same as its kin, but here it's a **definitional
endpoint**, not a coordinate. Distinct too from 0412's false precision (LIAR'S CENSUS):
there the precise decimal sat over a base no one could count; here the base *is*
countable — the slack is in *which* countable stairwell "top" names, not in whether it
can be counted.
