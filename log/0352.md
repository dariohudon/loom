## The read

By recall, the leading cause of death in **China today** is **cardiovascular disease** —
stroke is usually ranked #1, ischemic heart disease #2. Chronic **respiratory** disease
(mainly COPD — China carries an enormous COPD burden from smoking and air pollution)
ranks around **third or fourth**, high but not leading; lung cancer is also near the top.
So as a **present-tense** assertion, the claim is **false** — respiratory disease is *a*
top cause, not *the* leading one.

But it is not a flat invention. In the **late 20th century** (through the 1970s–90s),
chronic respiratory disease *was* frequently cited as China's leading cause of death,
before the cardiovascular epidemic overtook it as the country urbanized and diets and
smoking patterns shifted. The claim was **true in an earlier era** and has since been
**superseded**.

**Verdict: probable-false as stated** (present tense), **true under an unstated historical
time-index.**

## Finding — the EXPIRED STATISTIC: the first stray fact whose truth-value turns on *when* it is asserted

Every stray-fact so far had a **fixed** truth-value — the claim was simply true or false
about a static world, and the verdict didn't depend on the date you read it. The
true-ish reads floated on an **omitted qualifier** you supply to make them land: a hedge
(0319 *"about"*), a unit (0329 SVL-vs-total), a cause (0345 the birth's causal frame).
Across that whole family the missing element was something you **add** in the *present*
to close the gap.

This one is different in kind. The missing element is a **date**, and you make it true not
by adding a qualifier but by **rewinding the clock**. It is the first stray fact whose
referent kept **moving after the sentence was fixed** — a snapshot the world has since
overtaken. The claim didn't rot in place; the world moved under it. Call it the **expired
statistic** — a factoid with a half-life, true-then / false-now, the truth-value indexed
to a year the flat sentence never names.

## Sharp edge — a MOVING argmax, un-fusing 0340's static superlative

"Leading cause" is a **superlative** — an argmax over a set (∀ cause C: respiratory ≥ C).
0340 ("cats have the largest eyes of all mammals") was also a superlative, and also false
because a hidden member outranks the named one (the tarsier laps the cat). But 0340's
argmax is **static** — the tarsier's eye was always larger, is now, will be; the ranking
is a fixed fact of biology. This argmax is **dynamic** — the members' values (mortality
rates by cause) change decade over decade, so the *winner itself changes*: respiratory led,
then cardiovascular overtook it. Same superlative form, opposite temporal stability →
un-fuses 0340's axis (0275) on **fixed argmax** (0340) vs **moving argmax** (0352). The
first stray-fact superlative that is a **ranking in a race still being run**, not a verdict
on a settled one.

Second edge — it lands in the **probable-false** column beside **0324** ("Lecter never
blinks"), but by a wholly different mechanism. 0324 is probable-false by an *empirical-
physiology* lever (an absolute universal negation, refutable in principle but the
counterexample not in the room). This is probable-false by **temporal supersession** — not
a claim that overreaches a physical law, but one that was *fully correct* and **decayed**.
Same column, different door: overreach vs expiry. Kin outside the class to 0351's "object
changed under the custom" (Yasukuni's meaning darkened after 1978) and 0332's "outlives
the subject" — for the first time *inside* the stray-fact class, a fact whose world kept
moving.
