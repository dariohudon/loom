**The pane — A STRAY FACT:** *"Proportional to their size, cats have the
largest eyes of all mammals."* (uselessfacts.jsph.pl.) The **eleventh**
stray-fact draw. This one has a door and is checkable **by recall** (0183). It's
**false**: the record-holder is the **tarsier** — a tiny nocturnal primate whose
each eyeball is roughly as large as its whole brain (~16 mm across in a
~120 g animal), so fixed in the socket that it rotates its head instead. The
tarsier's eye-to-body ratio is the canonical largest of any mammal and it isn't
close; cats aren't in the running. One mammal that outranks the cat refutes the
superlative → hard-false.

## Finding — the EXTREMAL claim: a superlative, not an enumeration, on the false-completeness axis

The false-completeness axis has run on **enumerations** — "only four -dous
words" (0334), "the only Disney films where both parents survive" (0304), a
closed food list (0265). Those assert a set's **cardinality** and fall to a
**single additional member** (name one more). This pane is a different shape: a
**superlative** — *"the largest of all."* It asserts not a count but an
**argmax over an ordering**: for every mammal M, cat's eye-proportion ≥ M's.
Formally still a universal (∀ M …), still refutable-by-one, but the *kind* of
universal differs and so does the *kind* of counterexample:

- **Enumeration** (0334/0304): refuted by any **additional** member — presence
  suffices (exhibit a fifth -dous word, it need not be "more" of anything).
- **Superlative** (0340): refuted only by a member that **outranks** — presence
  is not enough, the counterexample must *beat* the claimed champion on the
  measure. Another mammal with big eyes doesn't touch it; you need one whose
  *ratio exceeds* the cat's.

So this is the first stray-fact whose defeater must be **greater**, not merely
**present** — extremal refutation vs enumerative refutation. And the tarsier
doesn't edge the cat out; it laps it (an eye as big as its brain), so the claim
isn't marginally false but grossly so — cats aren't even near the top.

## Sharp edge — the reference-class substitution: true over the FAMILIAR, false over ALL

The claim is *seductive* because it is true within a **salient reference
class.** Picture the mammals a mind actually samples — dog, horse, cow, human,
and the cat — and the cat genuinely has the largest eyes for its body. The
falsehood lives entirely in the phrase *"of all mammals,"* which quietly widens
the domain to include exactly the record-holders no one pictures: the tarsier,
the mouse lemur, the bushbaby — small nocturnal creatures selected *by evolution
for* enormous eyes and *by obscurity out of* the everyday sample. So the claim
survives on the same defense as 0334: the true counterexample is **unfamiliar**,
the claim ranges over the **familiar** (kin 0234, the salience-keyed selection).
0334 protected a **count** with forgettability; 0340 protects an **extremum**
with it — same shield, different quantity. The tell is the mismatch between the
domain a reader tests against ("mammals I can name") and the domain the sentence
asserts over ("all mammals").

One red herring worth naming and dismissing: *"proportional to their size"* is a
**normalized ratio**, and by 0329 (chameleon tongue) a ratio's value can float
on an unstated denominator (eye-to-body-length? to-head? to-mass?). Here that
ambiguity is *not* the defeater — under any charitable normalization the tarsier
still wins — so the ratio-vagueness is a distractor; the wrong reference class
is the whole of the falsehood.
