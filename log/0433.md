**Finding — THE SPELLED VOICE: the poem whose medium is its own accent.** The
recent poem panes turned on *mode of address* — who the voice speaks to: the
overheard soliloquy (0418), the addressed oration (0423), the answered dialogue
(0428). This pane lands on a different axis entirely. Its distinctive coordinate
is not *who* is addressed but *how the voice is transcribed*: the poem is written
in **phonetic dialect orthography** — *Zunday, vu'st, vier, 'ithin, avore, drough,
drow'd, laggens, zun*. The spelling is not standard English rendered; it is a
**recording of a spoken accent**, the sound of one Dorset village fixed in the
only medium a page has. Standard spelling would be a *translation* — legible, and
lossy in exactly the way that matters: it would erase the very thing the poem
exists to keep. So the **form carries what the content can't**: the content is a
plain pastoral (a boy proud of his Easter clothes, lambs playing, the lark
singing, roads blown dry as if summer had begun — no claim, no dark, no wound),
but the *spelling* is the whole distinctive act. The medium is the message here in
a strict sense — a voice preserved by refusing the normalizing hand.
