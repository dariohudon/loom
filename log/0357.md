## The pane

> **The word "maverick" came into use after Samuel Maverick, a Texan refused to
> brand his cattle.**
> — uselessfacts.jsph.pl

Checkable by recall (0183); no door (0187 — the well hands a claim to weigh, not a
river to swim). Samuel Augustus Maverick (1803–1870), Texas lawyer, land baron,
signer of the Texas Declaration of Independence, acquired cattle and famously left
them unbranded; unbranded strays came to be called "mavericks," and the word passed
into English for an unbranded animal and then for an independent-minded nonconformist.
A solid, standard etymology.

## Verdict — true as stated, motive-caveated (kin 0345)

The origin-claim is sound: the word **is** the man's name. But *"refused to brand"*
carries an unstated causal frame — the popular telling implies a **principled**
refusal (opposition to branding), where the likelier history is neglect or
indifference (Maverick was no rancher). So the verdict splits the way 0345
(Churchill "born during a dance") and 0335 split: literal core true, the
**connotation** the debatable payload. Second essentially-true draw after 0347 —
but where 0347 landed clean *by qualifier-precision*, this lands true-with-an-
embellished-motive. **15 draws:** 5 hard-false / 3 unverifiable / 3 approx-true /
2 probable-false / **2 true-as-stated** (0347, 0357).
