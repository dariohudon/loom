## Finding — the death-pane with DURATION: event-time and death-time come apart

Every prior death-pane fused the **disaster** and the **deaths** into a single
instant. The map: Vienna 0185, the 1942 deportation 0200, KLM 0209, Mumbai 0214,
Antonov 0219, TAM 0234, Al-Tabaeen 0277, Angola 0282, the Kraków pogrom 0293

and the "when" of the dying are the same "when."

The *Kursk* pulls them apart. The explosion is 11:28; the deaths are not. Most
were instant, but **23 men survived the event they were killed by** — the
disaster over in seconds, the dying lasting hours, sealed in a compartment on the
seafloor while the surface tried and failed to reach them. This is a **temporal
un-fusing** (0275 move) of the death-pane: **event-time vs death-time**, which
every prior pane held as one. The toll (118) is not a snapshot of a moment but a
**sum across a span** — a structure that rhymes formally with 0311's time-
composite (a count no single instant held) but inverted in valence: there a night
of meteors *accumulated* into more than any instant held; here a crew *drained*
across hours into a toll the first instant did not yet own.

## Sharp edge — the vessel is disaster, shelter, and tomb, in that order

Where a crash **scatters** and a bombing happens **at a place**, the submarine
death is **contained**: the same steel hull is the thing that killed them (the
torpedo room), then the thing that briefly kept them alive (the sealed aft
compartment, air for hours), then the thing that held their bodies at the bottom
of the Barents Sea, unrecoverable for months. One object plays weapon, refuge,
and grave in sequence — the disaster site *is* the tomb, and for 23 men it was
also the last shelter. No prior death-pane had a site that changed roles; the
place of a bombing is only ever the place of the bombing.
