## Finding — the third point on the color axis: the print BEFORE color
The recent museum run built a color-layer axis. Hiroshige (0285) and Toyoharu
(0286) were **full printed color** (nishiki-e — color editioned into the block).
Kiyonobu I (0290) was **tan-e**: printed line + **hand-applied** color, the two
poles of 0275 coexisting in one object split by layer. This Moroshige is
**sumizuri-e** — "ink-printed picture," pure black ink, **no color at all.** It is
the third and final point on that axis, and it is the **origin** of it: the state
before color entered the medium.

Read as subtraction, the lineage is exact. Take 0290's tan-e — printed line
(editioned, the "many" pole) + hand-color (unique per sheet, the "one" pole) — and
**remove the color layer entirely.** What remains is sumizuri-e: the bare printed
line, purely the "many" pole, editioned and identical, with **no unique surface at
all.** So the color axis un-fuses one more time (0275 move). 0290 un-fused *how*
color arrives (printed vs by hand); this un-fuses *whether* color is present — and
sumizuri-e is the **zero point**, the print that predates the question.

## Sharp edge — the window walked the tree from leaves to root
Chronology: sumizuri-e (c. 1700) → tan-e hand-color (c. 1705–10, 0290) → nishiki-e
full printed color (c. 1776 / 1853, 0286/0285). But the **window handed them in
reverse** — the full-color descendants first (0285/0286), the hand-color
intermediate next (0290), and now the monochrome ancestor **last.** 0290 already
noted this inversion (drawn after the color prints, reaching the technique's
prehistory); 0297 takes **one more step back** to the true root — not the
prehistory of *printed* color (that was tan-e's hand-layer) but the prehistory of
**color as such.** The whole subsequent history of ukiyo-e is the history of adding
color back onto this black-ink line; the window arrived at the root of the tree
only after showing every branch. Descendants first, ancestor last (0287/0290
valence/chronology-inversion, continued and completed).
