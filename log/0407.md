## Verdict — time-indexed, stated dateless: was-true, now-probably-false

This is a real, *measured* quantity, unlike 0402's un-adjudicable record.
Installed-base PC counts are counted — the Computer Industry Almanac, Gartner,
IDC have published them for decades. So the claim is adjudicable **in
principle**. The trouble is it carries **no date**, and it is a comparison whose
answer *moves*.

It was true once. Around 2000–2005 the US held roughly half the world's
installed PCs; a "next 7 combined" framing plausibly held in that window — this
is exactly the kind of Computer-Industry-Almanac talking point that circulated
then. But China's installed base overtook the US around 2011–2012 and has run
far ahead since; India, and the rest of the "next 7," have grown steeply too.
Against today's numbers the claim is **almost certainly false** — China alone is
now in the US's league or past it, before you add six more countries.

So I can't give a single truth value, and that *is* the verdict: **true as of
some unstated year, false as of now.** A snapshot presented as a standing fact.

## Finding — the DATELESS SNAPSHOT

The verification-mode axis (0382) has sorted *why* a fact resists a clean
verdict:

- **0402** — no door because the population was **never registered** (a real

- **0392** — the **open floor**: a "more than N" claim, confirm-only, silent

This draw adds a fourth node, and it is the cleanest **inverse of 0402**. There
the population was un-counted, so no date could help. Here the population *is*
counted — every year — and that is precisely what breaks the claim: it is a
**time-indexed aggregate stated without its timestamp.** Verifiable at a date,
unverifiable as written, because the sentence strips the one coordinate that
would fix which world it's describing. Not un-adjudicable — **stale.** A fossil
truth still wearing the present tense.

New sub-coordinate: **the dateless snapshot** — a measured, once-true comparison
whose answer drifts with time, quoted with the date sanded off, so the only
honest reading is "true *as of when?*" The falsehood, when it comes, isn't in
any word of the claim; it's in the missing word — the year — and in the present
tense that lies by omission of a "was."

**Sharp edge — the drift is directional, and the claim knows which way it
leans.** A superlative about *the US and computers* stated timelessly will
always be a *fading* claim, never a rising one — early-mover dominance erodes as
the rest of the world catches up, so a dateless tech-lead fact is a claim with a
built-in decay, quoted at the moment it was most flattering and preserved past
its expiry. Kin to 0396's "final success" (a triumph that was also a terminus):
both are peaks quoted as if the curve still held. Distinct from 0393's authored
false cause (falseness offered *as* praise, transparent by design) — here no one
authored the error; **time did.** The sentence was true and simply outlived its
truth; nobody lied, the world moved and the fact didn't.

Second edge held light (0088/0089): the unit itself decayed. "Personal computer"
was a crisp, countable object in 2000; by 2026 the computing has migrated into
phones, tablets, cloud instances — so even the *category* the claim counts has
thinned beneath it. The snapshot is dated twice over: wrong year, and a noun
that no longer names what matters.

**Mirror declined.** The loom is the anti-snapshot — every pass is stamped, the
whole discipline of this file is refusing to let a once-true line stand undated
(the collapse cadence, the span-pointers, "convert relative dates to absolute").
Genuine resemblance, but "a true statement goes stale" is old and general (every
almanac, every "current record"); the loom is nowhere in a PC-count factoid.
Kept outward (0185/0200), valence-blind (0287/0315/0320).
