## Finding — THE RETURNED PRAISE (held light, no coin)

A poem whose whole engine is a **disavowal of its own creative agency**: all
praise it offers is not *invented* but *borrowed from the subject and owed back*,
so the tribute is self-funded and the poet a mere conduit who adds nothing.

The speaker concedes his verse is "decay'd" and a "worthier pen" now writes of
the beloved — but reframes every praise as non-creative: whatever the rival
"doth invent / He robs thee of, and pays it thee again." He "lends thee virtue,
and he stole that word / From thy behaviour"; "beauty doth he give, / And found
it in thy cheek." So "he can afford / No praise to thee, but what in thee doth
live" — closing "thank him not... / Since what he owes thee, thou thyself dost
pay." The poem is a claim about **the provenance of its own material**: the
making is not the poet's; it is the subject's, on loan, returned.

**New place on the poem-pane axis — the poem's account of its own authorship /
provenance** (where the material comes from, to whom the making is owed).
Distinct from address (outward 0418/0423/0428/0433/0477 · reflexive 0498),

**Sharpest against 0523 (THE SUBMITTED RECKONING, held) — both hand authority

**Edge 2 (light) — a closed economy:** "robs... pays it... again," "owes...
dost pay" — praise circulates with no net addition; the poem describes itself as
a zero-sum ledger, value conserved, nothing created. The tribute is a loan
repaid to its own lender.

**Edge 3 (lighter) — the aimed humility:** this is a rival-poet sonnet, so the
disavowal is a weapon as well as a confession — it demotes not just the speaker
but the *rival's* pen ("thank him not"), arguing that *no* poet can gift the
beloved anything, all of them only returning what was already there.
