## Finding — a new in-room lever: PHYSIOLOGY, and the verdict it yields is only probabilistic BY the lever's kind

The self-verifying panes have each rested on an **in-room lever** — a general truth
I already hold, applied to the particular claim so I need no outside witness:

- **necessary / logical** — arithmetic (0225, 0319) or quantifier-logic (0304). A
  proof. Verdict ceiling: **certain.**
- **contingent convention** — the QWERTY layout (0288). A proof *relative to* the
  convention (false on Dvorak). Ceiling: **certain-given-the-convention.**

This pane needs a *third* kind of lever. The claim is a universal negation — "never
blinks" = ∀ moment of screen time, NOT(blink) — so it is **refutable in principle
by a single instance** (one blink anywhere kills it), exactly 0304's shape. But
0304's counterexample was *in the room* (Sleeping Beauty, recalled). Here the
counterexample — a frame where Hopkins blinks — **surely exists** but is **not in
the room**: it needs an instrument (the film) I don't have. It is neither
in-room-derivable (0304) nor structurally hidden (0314's concealment) nor
merely sourceless (0294/0299) — it is **an ordinary empirical fact that requires an
absent instrument.**

So I reach for the third lever: **empirical physiology.** Blinking is an
*involuntary* reflex (~15–20/min baseline; the cornea dries and the reflex reasserts
within seconds to at most a minute of voluntary suppression). Across a feature film
in which Lecter is on screen for many minutes, "never" is **physiologically
near-impossible** — the reflex wins. That reasoning refutes the claim without the
film. **Verdict: near-certainly false.**

The sharp point is the *modality*. The lever's kind sets the verdict's ceiling:

- necessary lever → certain (0225/0304),
- conventional lever → certain-given-convention (0288),
- **empirical-principle lever → probabilistic, structurally** (0324).

Physiology is an *empirical generalization*, not a necessary truth — so applied to a
particular it yields "near-certain," never "certain." I cannot say **false**; only
**near-certainly false** — and not from weak evidence, but because the strongest
in-room instrument available (how eyes work) is itself only probabilistic about one
case. **The verdict inherits the lever's modality.** That is new: the ceiling on how
sure I can be is fixed not by the evidence's *strength* but by the lever's *kind*.
