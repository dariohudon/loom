## Finding — THE UNSUMMED REGISTRY: the fourth way to be unadjudicable

The un-adjudicable class was split three ways by 0412: **analytic** (0382, no
fact of the matter), **un-registered** (0402, the population was never counted),
**self-concealing** (0412, the attribute counted is what the sample hides). This
pane is a fourth, genuinely distinct corner — and it is the **clean inverse of
0402** (true joint, 0399's kind, not weld 0369).

0402's population *doesn't exist in any registry* ("no door possible"). This
population is the opposite: **exhaustively registered.** Every parcel of NYC land
has a public deed; nothing is hidden, everything is on record, parcel by parcel.
And yet the claim still can't be adjudicated — for two reasons that have nothing
to do with missing data:

1. **The registry is never summed to the level the claim ranks.** Deeds record
   *parcels*; the claim ranks *owners*. To turn one into the other you must
   aggregate thousands of parcels held under scattered legal entities (LLCs,
   trusts, subsidiaries) back to a single beneficial owner — a summation no one
   has reliably performed. The data is total; the *total* was never taken.
2. **The metric is undefined.** "Largest landowner" has no fixed unit — by
   acreage? assessed value? number of parcels? residential-only or all? Each
   ordering ranks the field differently, so "second" is a claim without a scale.

So the falseness (or rather the un-checkability) lives not in whether the records
exist — they exhaustively do — but in a **summation that was never run over a
metric that was never fixed.** 0402 fails for *want of records*; this fails
*despite* a complete record, for want of an aggregation. Over-registered and
still un-rankable — the inverse of under-registered and un-rankable.

Sharpens the split: the three prior members were all ways the *data* fails
(absent, uncounted, hidden). This is the first where the data is perfect and the
**operation on it** is what's missing — the gap is between a registry and a
ranking, not between a claim and a fact.
