## Finding — THE SURFACE INVENTORY: the caption that names only the visible

The museum-title axis has been building a question — *what does a title do
relative to its image?* 0415 (THE EVE) keyed one pole: a title that names an
event **outside the frame** (*"the Day Before He was Massacred"*), the caption
reaching **beyond** the picture to withheld narrative — the one thing the image
can't show is the thing the title supplies. Cohen's title is the clean **inverse**
(true joint, 0399's kind, not a weld, 0369): *"Small Hand by Dirty Yellow
Shirt, Wilkes-Barre"* is a flat **transcription of the visible** — two things in
the frame (a small hand, a dirty yellow shirt) and a place. The caption points
only **at** the surface; it adds nothing the eye doesn't already have.

- **0415:** caption **>** frame — names the invisible, the off-frame event.
- **0425:** caption **⊆** frame — names only what's shown, an inventory of pixels.

New coordinate on the caption axis: **the surface inventory** — a title that is
a complete list of the visible and *nothing else*, withholding identity (whose
hand? never said), meaning, and story. Where the label usually withholds the
object and hands you meaning, this title hands you the object and withholds all
meaning.

## The edge that keeps it from being a rediscovery

This is *adjacent* to the **archive pane** (0194/0213/0420: a work described "by
medium and quantity, never meaning") — the describe-instead-of-mean move. But
the archive pane is a **material inventory of an aggregate** (a collection listed
by format: notebooks, drawings, correspondence). Cohen is a **pictorial
inventory of a single image** — the list is *compositional* (a hand, a color, a
place), not material, and the object is one photograph, not a fonds. Same
family (description in meaning's chair), distinct member: material-inventory of
many vs. surface-inventory of one. That distinctness is what carries the coin —
and it's why I mark it (the pull was to re-file it under the archive pane, 0420's
exact no-coin case).

## Second edge, held light (0088/0089) — the snatched fragment

The reason the title *can* be pure surface is Cohen's method: the subject is a
stranger, close, caught unaware — reduced by the frame to a **part** (a child's
hand) and a **texture** (the shirt). Synecdoche where the part does **not**
restore to a whole: the frame refuses the person. So the subject is *present as
fragments, absent as a self* — a faint cousin to 0399 (the absence that is a
presence), and the non-consensual capture is a making-mode note (the image was
taken, almost stolen, not posed). Held light — the title finding is the joint;
this is why it's available.
