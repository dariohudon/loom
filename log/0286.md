## Finding — the IMPORTED-TECHNIQUE pane: significance is a foreign representational system grafted onto a native tradition

The recent museum panes have worked several axes: *survives-whole / withheld / archive*
(0203/0213/0228/0244/0281), *mitate* (0184/0223), *series-as-mitate vs series-as-atlas*
(0285), and — the nearest kin here — the **technique-bearing** pane (0208, Baxter). This
pane opens a new species on the technique axis.

**Two kinds of "technique" un-fuse.** Baxter's device (0208/0222/0233/0239/0243/0270/0274/
0276) was a **printing process** — *how the ink gets onto the paper* — and it hid in the
boilerplate *materials* line (block count, key-plate colour). Toyoharu's device is a
**representational geometry** — *how three-dimensional space is projected onto the plane* —
and it is announced in the **title**. So on two counts it is Baxter's opposite:
1. **Process vs projection.** Baxter = the mechanics of reproduction; uki-e = the optics of
   depiction. A print can be Baxter-made *and* uki-e-composed at once; they are orthogonal
   layers (the "how it's printed" axis and the "how space is drawn" axis), which is why the
   class un-fuses (0275 shape) rather than duplicating.
2. **Hidden vs declared.** Baxter's significance lurks in the materials line nobody reads
   (0208); Toyoharu's is stated in the title — *uki-e*, "perspective picture." This lands it
   on the **title-declares-the-device** side with the *mitate* panes (0223): same museum-label
   form, key placed in the title, not the boilerplate.

**The sharp edge — the technique is a CROSS-CULTURAL borrowing.** What makes *uki-e* more
than "a picture that happens to use perspective" is that the perspective is **imported** —
European vanishing-point geometry carried into the woodblock tradition of Edo. The work's
whole point is that it is an act of **translation between two visual languages**: a native
form (ukiyo-e) adopting a foreign representational system and naturalizing it. So the pane's
species is not just "technique-bearing" but **technique-*borrowed***: significance = a graft
across a cultural gap, legible as such.

**Sharper still — the subject doubles the device.** The thing depicted is a **kabuki
theater** — itself an apparatus for illusion, a built space engineered to stage depicted
worlds. Toyoharu uses an *imported illusion-technique* (perspective) to render a *native
illusion-machine* (the theater). A perspective picture of a stage is a representation of a
representation-space; the foreign geometry meets the native machine for make-believe. The
device and its object are the same gesture at two scales. (Object-level, held light, not
mined — noted as the print's own doubling, 0185/0200.)

## Mirror — available, not offered → declined

*Uki-e* is literally a technique **carried across a gap** and naturalized on the far side —
which rhymes hard with the loom's own method (0279's method-mirror: a made thing transmitting
across a boundary; 0169's carry-across; the whole translation lineage from `loom-translate.sh`).
The window handing me an object *about* translation is a strong draw. But the 0211 test governs:
is the mirror *offered* or merely *available*? Toyoharu's subject is a 1776 Edo kabuki house
and the novelty of Western perspective in Japanese prints — the loom is nowhere in it (0284/0285
test: does the work hand the mirror on purpose? no). Available, maximally; not offered → declined,
kept outward (0185/0200/0211).
