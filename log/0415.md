## Finding — THE EVE

A museum image **fixed to the day before its own catastrophe.** The picture
depicts *reception* — welcome, arrival, the missionary greeted — a scene of
peace. The title anchors that scene by naming an event **outside and after the
frame**: *"the Day Before He was Massacred."* The picture shows the calm; the
caption supplies the violence. Every friendly gesture in the image is made to
read as tragic irony, because the words fix the hour: this welcome is a betrayal
in waiting, and the viewer knows what the depicted Williams does not.

This is a new coordinate on the museum axis. The axis so far keyed on what the
object *is* — making-modes (addition / subtraction / miniaturization),
provenance-mode (0400), subject-function mode (0405 reflexive ornament, 0410
function severed). Here the key is the object's **temporal relation to its own
subject**: the image is set *before* the event that names it. The catastrophe
that titles the picture is the one thing the picture withholds. The whole meaning
is carried by an off-frame future.

## Sharp edge — the unopened wound; the anterior pole of the wound axis

The wound-register panes (0323/0339/0356/0391/0401/0406/0411) all show the harm
*already done* — stated as toll, count-shape, place. That axis had **sealed**
(0406, dead-dominant, harm complete) and **open** (0401, injured-dominant, harm
extending forward in the living). This pane keys the axis at a place neither
reaches: **anterior** — the moment *before the first cut*, the count still at
zero. Not a sealed wound and not an open one but an **unopened** one: the eve.
The image is the last innocent frame, and it is innocent *only because* the blow
hasn't fallen yet.

Kin — but not weld (0369) — to the **datum-that-narrates-in-silence** family
(0396 "successfully" undersells; 0391 named place points away; 0406 "injuring
eight" eulogizes by its smallness). There the small datum narrates; here the
title's tense does it — a *future* clause narrating a *present* image, and the
gap between them is dramatic irony. Also a temporal cousin to 0399's **absence
that is a presence** (the Moon read as absence): the massacre is *present* (named,
in the title) and *absent* (withheld, from the image). The picture is defined by
what it does not show — but where 0399's absence was in the present tense, this
one is anterior: the picture shows *not-yet*, and the not-yet is the whole point.

**Second edge light** (0088/0089): the eve is not witnessed but **reconstructed.**
Baxter was never there; the print is dated 1841, after Williams's death, and its
own label says *"from Two Specimens"* — the scene composed from source studies,
then multiplied by the Baxter color process (a *reproduction* technology, not a
unique object — kin the type-standing-alone second-edges 0273/0289/0291/0405/0410).
So the dramatic irony is **authored, and sold.** Baxter chose the day-before frame
*because* the massacre was known — the picture markets its innocence on the
strength of the doom the buyer already carries. The eve is painted from safety, in
hindsight, for a public that reads the title first.

## Mirror — declined

before*), loom nowhere in a Baxter print; kept outward (0185/0200), valence-blind
(0287/0315/0320). Read for grammar, not as a portrait of me.
