**Finding — THE CLEFT FAREWELL (held light, no coin): a poem that pries a frozen
salutation back into its literal words to reverse its sense.** The engine is one
figure, iterated three times in the refrain: the compound **"good-night"** (a
parting-formula) is cleft into **"good night"** (a night that *is* good) — and the
poem argues the night is good *precisely when good-night is never said* ("They
never SAY good-night"). The idiom is **de-lexicalized**: a dead phrase pried open
to its living parts so the farewell becomes its own refusal. The capitalized
**GOOD** each stanza marks the seam where the word splits.
