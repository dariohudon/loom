## Finding — the JOINT archive: a finding aid of TWO lives fused into one collection

This is an **archive-pane**, and that class is well-mapped. Twice it arrived as a
**finding aid** — Martyl Papers (0194/0218), Don Baum Papers (0213): a record
that describes a life **by medium and quantity, never meaning**, and so
**withholds the life** (0213). Once it arrived inside-out — the "70 Plus" oral
history (0228): catalogs the **account in the subject's own voice**, and so **is
nothing but the meaning**. 0228 un-fused the archive on **what is cataloged**
(medium vs voice).

The Yost and Taylor Collection is a **finding aid** again — its materials line is
pure medium-enumeration (papers, correspondence, prints, slides, drawings,
reprographic prints, scrapbooks), the 0213 form exactly, so it lands on the
withholds-the-life side. What's **new is not *what* it catalogs but *whose*:** it
is the **first joint archive** — two named people fused under one collection
name, "Yost and Taylor." So the axis un-fuses once more (0275 move) on a property
the prior three didn't touch:

- **single-subject archive** (Martyl 0194, Baum 0213) — one life, withheld.
- **joint-subject archive** (0307) — **two** lives fused into one record.

## Sharp edge — the fusion is doubly effacing

A finding aid already withholds the **meaning** of one life (0213: medium and
quantity, never why). A **joint** finding aid does that **and** erases the
**seam** between the two: from a bare medium-list you cannot recover **whose**
correspondence, **whose** drawing, **whose** scrapbook — the partition itself is
gone. Where a single-subject aid withholds one life, this withholds two lives
**and the boundary between them.** And the form **enacts its subject**: these
were architects, presumably partners; a record of a **partnership** that fuses
two people so completely into one collection that even the line dividing them is
no longer legible. The record's shape *is* the practice's shape — two made one.

## Second note (held light) — makers of permanent things, kept only in their paper

The subjects **built buildings** — durable made-things meant to outlast their
makers. Yet the archive preserves only the **ephemeral working residue**:
correspondence, slides, scrapbooks, *reprographic* prints (working copies, not
the works). An archive of makers-of-the-permanent that keeps the **paper, not the
buildings** — the inverse of the museum label, which withholds the object and
hands the meaning (0213); here the object (the buildings) is out in the world and
the archive holds the **traces of the making**. Held light (0185/0200), not
pushed.

The "**bulk 1935–1965**" note is the finding aid announcing its own **temporal
density** — 66-year span, 30-year core where the mass lies — the archivist's
version of density-not-run (0275/0276) and portrait-vs-census (0296). Also held
light.
