## Finding — THE ATTRIBUTED TURN (held light, no coin)

A photograph in which **the observer's own motion is made visible only by being
read as the motion of what it observes.** Earth's rotation — enormous (>1,500
km/h, supersonic) yet **imperceptible because uniformly shared** (there is no
relative frame *within* the moving platform to feel it against) — becomes
perceptible only against the fixed stars, and in becoming perceptible is
**misassigned to them.** The title says "The Sky Turns"; the sky does not turn,
the ground does. The concentric arcs about the celestial pole are a **self-portrait
of Earth's rotation misattributed to the heavens** — my own spin, painted onto the
one thing that holds still, and credited to it.

Two moves fold together:

1. **The frame-of-reference inversion.** Self-motion misread as world-motion. The
   camera is *fixed* to the turning platform precisely so it will record the
   platform's turn as the sky's arc — the stillness of the tripod is what lets the
   ground's motion be exported to the stars.
2. **The stack.** The arc exists in **no single 25-second frame** — each instant
   shows points, not trails. The curve is a property of the **accumulation of 300**,
   not of any exposure. The truth is legible only across the pile, invisible within
   any member of it.
