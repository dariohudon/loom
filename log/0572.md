## Finding — THE JURISDICTIONAL CONSTANT (held light, no coin)

**A contingent, jurisdiction-varying convention presented in the grammar of a
fixed universal standard — "a *regulation* golf ball" — that betrays its own
over-claim by immediately supplying two national numbers, since a genuine
standard does not have a nationality.** The word "regulation" borrows the
authority of a *statute* (a count fixed by rule, necessary, the same
everywhere); the very next clause forks that count by country. A thing that is
336 here and 330 there was not *mandated* to be either — it was *chosen*, and
the choice tracked a local convention. The precision is real (dimples are
discrete, you can count them); the **modality** is false (nothing *requires*
the number, so "regulation" is doing work it hasn't earned).

**The tell is entirely internal, no recall needed.** "Regulation" is singular
and absolute; "US ... UK" is plural and contingent. Put them in one breath and
they refute each other: if it were truly regulated, one number would rule both
jurisdictions; if two numbers stand, then "regulation" names not a law but a
**local custom dressed as one.** A constant with a dialect is not a constant.

**Two seams:**
1. **Borrowed necessity** — "regulation" imports the *feel* of a mandate onto a
   manufacturer's free choice. The number is an engineering decision (a
   dimple pattern that fits a given ball diameter), not a rule; the word
   launders a *convention* as a *requirement*.
2. **The fork is the confession** — the two values aren't noise around a
   standard, they are evidence there *is* no single standard for this quantity.
   The honest fossil underneath (a real US/UK ball-*size* divergence, unsworn)
   would explain why two numbers exist — but the pane relabels a difference of
   *diameter* as a difference of "regulation dimple count," freezing a contingent
