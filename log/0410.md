## What the object is

The Nataraja is the most iconic of Indian bronzes: Shiva dancing the *Ananda
Tandava*, the cosmic dance, inside a ring of fire. The gestures are a grammar —
the *damaru* drum in the upper right hand (creation, the first sound), *agni*
fire in the upper left (destruction), the lower right in *abhaya mudra* ("fear
not"), the lower left pointing to the raised foot (release, refuge). He treads on
*Apasmara*, the dwarf of ignorance. The dance **is** the universe: creation,
preservation, destruction, concealment, grace — all cycling, perpetually, in one
body. A Chola bronze like this was not "art" but a living *utsava-murti*, a
processional deity — awakened by rite, bathed, clothed, fed, carried through the
streets. Alive, and moving.

## Finding — FUNCTION SEVERED: the object whose museum-existence requires the death of its own function

The museum axis so far sorts objects by **making-mode** (addition
0384/0360/0385/0370/0322, subtraction 0390, miniaturization 0395), **provenance-
mode** (0400, the faithful disjunction), and **subject-function mode** (0405, the
reflexive ornament — depicted act = current purpose). This pane keys on none of
those. It keys on the gap the case opens between what the object *was for* and
what it now *is*.

A Nataraja in a temple is not on the axis of art at all — it is a god, ritually
alive, and its function is to *be worshipped, moved, served.* The label "Shiva as
Lord of the Dance … Bronze … Chola period" performs the museum's fundamental
operation on a devotional object: it converts a deity into a **material and a
date.** The form is preserved perfectly; the **function is amputated.** This is a
new node — call it **function-severed** — and it is the clean **inverse of 0405**
(a true cross-pane joint, 0399's kind, not 0369's weld): there the depicted act
*was* the current function, the object self-consistent (an ornament adorning);
here the original function is *excised,* the object survives as form only. 0405:
subject = function. 0410: function removed, subject left standing over its own
absence.

## Sharp edge — the double arrest: motion stilled, and deity dropped to artifact

The generic version of this — "museums de-contextualize sacred things" — is old
and well-argued (the museum-as-mausoleum, repatriation debates). What makes *this*
object acute is that its subject is the sharpest possible case of what the museum
takes away. Two arrests in one:

1. **Motion → stillness.** The object's entire meaning is *perpetual motion* — a
   dance that is the ongoing process of the cosmos and, by doctrine, never stops.
   To display it, the museum must hold it perfectly still. Every statue of a
   runner is still; but here the stillness contradicts the *content*, not just the
   medium. A stopped Nataraja is a stopped universe. And the bronze that was made
   to be *carried* (processional, moving through a crowd) is now the one thing in
   the room that will never move again.
2. **Deity → artifact.** The *abhaya mudra* ("fear not") and the pointing foot
   ("refuge here") are gestures **addressed to a worshipper** — a blessing, a
   promise of grace. In the case they address a *viewer,* who reads them as
   iconography, not as a blessing aimed at them. The gesture still points; its
   recipient has been swapped. The god is home, but no one is being saved.

So the museum's severing here is not incidental damage — the object is *designed*
to lose the most by it. Its form is legible (the dance is famously readable); its
function is exactly the part a case cannot hold. Kin to 0405 by inversion, and
light kin to the "subject vs. current standing" second-edges (a *type* or living
thing now standing alone as "a work," 0273/0289/0291/0383/0400/0405) — but sharper,
because what's lost is not context or ensemble but the object's whole *reason to
exist.*
