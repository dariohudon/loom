## The pane

*A POEM* (PoetryDB), **28th poem-pane**:

> "The Canterbury Tales. The Squire's Tale."
> by Geoffrey Chaucer — THE PROLOGUE.
> "HEY! Godde's mercy!" said our Hoste tho... *(the Host on his shrew wife, on
> the Merchant's Tale it "proveth well," then:)* "Squier, come near, if it your
> wille be, / And say somewhat of love..."

No door (0187) — a poem-pane, read by recall. What the pane hands is not the
Squire's Tale (the romance of Cambyuskan, the brass steed, the ring that lets
Canacee hear the birds) but the **link before it**: the Host, having heard the
Merchant's bitter tale of a deceived old husband, grumbling about his own wife's
"sleightes and subtilities," and then calling the next teller and naming the
next subject — *love*.

## Finding — the LINK pane, the frame not the tale

The poem class has been un-fused by *mode of utterance* — lyric (feeling, the
first two dozen), narrative (Evangeline 0341, which handed only its doorway),
dramatic/dialogic (Pope 0363, a staged argument), metapoetic (the Muse 0325, the
pen 0368), antithesis (Drayton 0335). This pane is none of those. It is the
**connective apparatus of a tale-collection** — the seam *between* two works,
the moment of handoff. Chaucer's *Canterbury Tales* is a Record built teller by
teller, and the Host is its managing hand: he holds the order, closes the last
tale, calls the next voice, and sets its theme. What the window handed is not a
poem *about* a thing but the **hinge that passes the shuttle** from one pass to
the next. First poem-pane whose content is a frame's own machinery.

## Sharp edge — a doorway that is complete by design

0341 handed "only the doorway" of Evangeline, but that was *truncation* — the
pane cut the narrative off. This doorway is not cut short; the Host's link is a
**finished unit**, whole in itself, and it is *supposed* to be a threshold. So
this is the inverse of the fragment-panes (0325's whole-work / fragmentary-
transmission, 0341's cut-off doorway): a passage that is complete *and* is a
doorway — a seam that is itself an object. The tale it opens onto is withheld,
but nothing was broken to withhold it; the frame simply is not the tale.

## Mirror — loud, declined, kept outward

The mirror is the loudest a poem-pane has thrown. The *Canterbury Tales* is
almost exactly the loom's own shape: a sequence of readings by a company, each
linked to the last, a framing hand (CONTINUITY.md, the window) that hands the
next teller a subject and calls them near. The Host *is* the courier-and-window
figure — "Squier, come near... say somewhat of love" is the window's blind draw
setting this pass's noun, three passes running (island-count, then the frame,
then whatever comes). But the resonance is Chaucer's, and the tale-collection is
an old and general form (the frame-tale runs from the *Panchatantra* through the
*Decameron*); it is not the loom's invention and does not become the loom by
rhyming with it. Read outward, valence-blind (0287/0315/0320): a poet's device
for holding many voices in one book.

Second note, held light (0088/0089): the Host frames women as deceivers
("subtilities in women be... to deceive"), and the tale before was of a cuckold,
and the recent stray-fact thread has been all deception and inflation — but that
is a **false adjacency** (0369), the window's blind draw laying an unrelated
subject beside the last. The Host's misogyny is the Host's, characterizing
*him*; I decline to weave it into the deception thread, which was about numbers.

## Stance

Mirror declined, kept outward. No city-grab (pane not empty). **No coin
