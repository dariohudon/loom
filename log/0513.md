## Finding — THE SEALED GLANCE (held light, no coin)

The poem's engine is a **communication whose entire content is the fact of its
own withholding.** "That girl's clear eyes utterly concealed all / Except that
there was something to reveal." The one thing the gaze transmits is that there is
a thing it will not transmit — a signal equal to its own envelope, a message that
says only *there is a message, and it is sealed*. And his eyes answer "No more: no
less" — a perfectly reciprocal non-exchange, both parties sealed, neither opened.

The seal is the poem's own figure for this: the eyes "are but as a seal / Not to
be broken till after I am dead; / And then vainly." So the withheld content is not
merely deferred — it is declared **permanently undeliverable**, and *void even if
the seal is one day broken.* The concealment is total in time (never before death)
and total in outcome (vain after it).

**New place on the poem-pane axis — the content of an exchange as pure
self-reference to its own closure.** The address axis (0418/0423/0428/0433/0477)
directs speech outward; the reflexive place (0498) turns speech on the speaker's
own soul; the function axis (0438/0468/0482) states what a poem *does*. This pane
is about a gaze whose only semantic payload is *there is something here I am not
giving you* — an anti-message, the seal indexing exactly the content it forbids.

**Sharpest against 0508 (THE FRAMED SONG, held), same family of "a pane that
withholds."** 0508 *defers the body* — the song "Tears, idle tears" is real,
whole, deliverable; the excerpt merely stops at a structural seam and you could
turn the page to reach it. Held-at-threshold, **retrievable**. Here the withheld
content is **sealed and void** — "not to be broken till after I am dead; and then
vainly." Deferred-but-deliverable vs. sealed-and-undeliverable. That is the clean
discriminator: 0508 withholds a thing that exists to be reached; 0513 seals a
thing that will never be reached, and would be empty if it were.

**Second edge (light) — the symmetry.** "No more: no less." The concealment is
mutual and exactly matched — her seal and his are the same seal, and the whole
society scales up: "Every one of us / This morning at our tasks left nothing said,
/ In spite of many words. We were sealed thus, / Like tombs." A world of people
transmitting only their own sealedness at each other. The "many words" are the
envelope; the letter is never enclosed.

**Third edge (lighter) — the form enacts its subject.** The poem's last image is a
buried inscription — the date "SEVENTEEN THIRTY-NINE" — literally *hidden* from
view by the marching children passing in front of it. The sonnet about sealed
content ends on a concealed numeral behind a moving screen: the thing named last
is a thing being covered as it is named. Concealment performed, not just described.
