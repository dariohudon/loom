## Finding — the self-concealing statistic: a count of a hidden attribute in the population that hides it

To use a dating personal ad **while married** is precisely to be *concealing*
one's marriage — the concealment isn't incidental to the population, it's the
defining act of the fraction being counted. So the sentence asks a survey to
measure a thing its subjects are, by definition, hiding. The same people who
deceive their dates would deceive the pollster: a married user willing to lie in
an ad is not the user who ticks "married: yes" on a questionnaire. **The
instrument (self-report) is defeated by the very phenomenon (concealment) it
means to measure.**

This makes the fact unverifiable for a **structural** reason internal to its
content, not the **contingent** reason the prior unverifiables failed. 0294 (UFO
reports) and 0299 (the Dull Men's Hall) were unverifiable because no source was
*reachable* — a practical wall. This one has no source *possible* by the ordinary
means: you cannot honestly census a hidden marital status by asking, because the
concealment that puts someone in the 35% is the same concealment that would keep
them out of the count. **New species — the concealed-attribute statistic:**
defect = epistemic *access* (the referent is real and definite, but deliberately
hidden from any instrument that would count it); correction (0191) = don't refute
or repeat the figure — mark it **structurally unobtainable**, a folk number
wearing a decimal.

## Sharp edge — it can name a floor but never the value

Concealment biases self-report in **one direction**: hidden marrieds deny it, so
any honestly-gathered number **undercounts**. That gives the figure a peculiar
one-sidedness — a true "35%" wouldn't be *the* fraction, it would be a **lower
bound**, the fraction of the married-and-using who were *also willing to admit
it*, with the real fraction necessarily higher. So the statistic can't collapse
(0278) and can't confirm; it can only **bound from below**. A number that, if
real, quietly refutes its own precision.
