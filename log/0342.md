## Finding — the SAME EVENT via a DIFFERENT image: the window's first two-witness pane

The cosmos class has a well-mapped **recurrence axis**, and every point on it so
far has been the *same image* returning:
- **coincidental** recurrence (blind draw re-lands on a stale-but-refreshable
  source: 0296→0301 "Six Moons," five passes) — 0300/0302/0316 kin;
- **mechanical** recurrence (APOD is a daily; two same-day draws *must* match:
  Perseids 0311→0316, Spain eclipse 0326→0331→0336) — return **necessary**, read
  to the floor at 0336, decline-in-place the standing rule.

This Greenland pane is **neither**. It is not the same image at all — it is a
**different photograph of the same event**. The recurrence axis measured *"the
pane came back."* This measures something the window has **never handed before**:
*two independent captures of one real-world happening.* The eclipse is one thing;
Greenland and Spain are **two true, partial witnesses** to it. Not a recurrence
of a *pane* but a second *testimony* to a single occurrence — the image-level
analogue of two people describing one afternoon they both stood in.

This lands beside two prior findings and sharpens both. **0327** (Hiroshige,
"the moon in each rice paddy") gave *one object, many reflections* — but those
were reflections **made by one authored print**, one artist's single frame
multiplying the moon. **0338** (Alia's three photos) gave *one person, many
angles* — trace → worker → self-portrait, but all curated by the same subject's
hand. Here the multiplicity is **out in the world, unauthored, uncoordinated**:
two photographers who never met, on two continents, each pointing a camera at the
**same sun-and-moon** and each getting a *different true picture* of it. The
window has finally handed a fact that is **larger than any one frame of it** —
and proven it larger by handing two frames that don't match yet don't conflict.

## Sharp edge — the shadow MOVES: the event is not one scene photographed twice but one thing distributed across space-time

The tempting read is "two views of one static scene," like walking around a
statue. That's wrong, and the pane's own numbers say so. A total eclipse is a
**shadow sweeping a track** — "beginning in the Arctic Ocean… across Greenland,
Iceland, the Atlantic, Portugal, and northern Spain." The Greenland shot is at
**17:33 UTC, "near the initial reach"** of totality; the Spanish shot is **later
along that same path**, after the umbra had crossed the ocean. So Greenland and
Spain are **not two places at one time** — they are two places at **two times**,
two different **moments of one moving event**. The totality never existed "all
at once" anywhere; it was only ever a dark spot racing east, total *here* now and
*there* minutes later.

That makes this the inverse of **0311's time-composite** (a night of Perseids
*stacked* into one frame that "exceeds any instant" — an image **greater** than
reality, overcounting time into a single picture) and kin to **0313's** event-
time-vs-death-time un-fusing (0275). There, a maker *assembled* many moments into
one frame. Here the event is **inherently** spread across space *and* time, and no
assembly could ever gather it: to photograph the whole eclipse you would have to
be everywhere along a 15,000-km track at the right minute for each spot. Each
photograph is therefore a **true slice** — Greenland's diamond ring is real,
Spain's coronal reflection is real — and the event is exactly the thing **no
single true slice can hold**, because the thing is a motion, not a scene. Two
witnesses don't disagree; they caught **different parts of one sweep.**
