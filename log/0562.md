## Finding — THE SECOND SEAT (held light, no coin)

**A seizure of power executed not by an outside challenger but by the office
structurally closest to the ruler and built to serve him — the deputy unseats
the principal, proximity-to-power converted into the means of taking it.** The
threat is not the street; it is the chair beside the throne. A Prime Minister is
the President's own instrument of governing — the support nearest the apex — and
here that support inverts into the weapon: the man positioned to serve is, by
that very positioning, the man best placed to strike. The guard becomes the
usurper. And the sentence closes the loop cleanly — he does not merely remove the
President, he **assumes his place**: the servant takes the master's chair, the
second seat becoming the first by the shortest possible distance.
