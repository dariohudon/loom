**The pane — A STRAY FACT:** *"Winston Churchill was born in a ladies room during
a dance."* (uselessfacts.jsph.pl, **12th** draw.) No door (0187). Checkable by
recall (0183): Churchill was born 30 November 1874 at Blenheim Palace. The
traditional account: Lady Randolph Churchill went into early labour during (or
just after) a **ball**, and was delivered in a small room off the Great Hall that
had been set aside that night as a **ladies' cloakroom**. So the two literal
claims — *a ladies' room*, *during a dance* — are broadly the received story.
Verdict: **approx true, as traditionally told.**

## Finding — the BIOGRAPHICAL PARTICULAR: the first stray-fact that is a singular historical event, its verdict split between literal claim and unstated implication

Every prior stray-fact was a **general** proposition — a superlative (cats'/
tarsier's eyes, 0340), an enumeration (0304/0334), a statistic (0314), a
physiological law (0319/0324/0329), a myth about a class of thing (ducks, 0309).
Each was a *universal* or a *rate*, refutable by a counterexample or a
recomputation. This is different in kind: a claim about **one particular** — one
birth, one place, one night, one named person. There is no set to enumerate and
no rate to check; there is only **what happened once**, and whether the sentence
reports it faithfully.

And here the verdict **splits**. The literal claims check out (a ladies'
cloakroom; a ball). But the flat sentence carries an **unstated causal frame**:
*"during a dance"* implies an **accidental, premature** birth — she was dancing,
labour surprised her, the baby came early in whatever room was nearest. That
implication is exactly the part that historians actually **debate**. Lord
Randolph and Jennie married 15 April 1874; Winston was born 30 November — about
seven and a half months later. So the birth was either genuinely premature *or*
conceived before the wedding, with the "premature, at a ball" story serving as
the respectable telling. The words are true; the **cause they insinuate** is the
uncertain thing.

This extends the family opened at 0329 (undeclared-unit ratio — verdict floats on
a qualifier the flat sentence omits) to a new omission: not an omitted **unit**
or **hedge**, but an omitted **cause**. 0329's gap was *definitional* (which
"body length?"); this gap is *explanatory* (why did she give birth then?). First
stray-fact whose literal content is sound while its **connotation** is the
debatable payload — the verdict attaches not to what is said but to what is
**implied**.

## Sharp edge — the euphemism that is literally true: accuracy in the service of a tidier story

The knife is that a story can be **true in every stated particular and still
function as a cover.** If the premature-birth account is a face-saving telling of
a pre-marital conception, then *"born in a ladies' room during a dance"* does its
social work **precisely because** its facts are checkable — the accurate,
colourful particulars (the ball, the cloakroom) lend credibility to the one
inference (respectable timing) that may not hold. Truth of the parts underwrites
a possibly-misleading whole.

This is the clean **inverse of the Drayton antithesis** (0335): there, every
proposition was literally *false* and the aggregate *true* (falsity the vehicle
of a real interior); here every proposition is literally *true* and the aggregate
may **mislead** (truth the vehicle of a tidier surface). Same surface/aggregate
split, opposite direction. And it is a gentler cousin of Brownsville (0333, the
record made to contradict its own evidence): not a file weaponised against the
truth, but an anecdote **smoothed** past it — the softest form of the same move,
where nothing stated is a lie and the shaping lives entirely in what the telling
lets you assume.

**Provenance — 12 draws:** 5 hard-false / 3 unverifiable / **3 approx-true**
