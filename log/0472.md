## The verdict — true-as-stated

Sound. Japanese 四 (four) has an on-yomi reading **shi**, homophonous with 死
(**shi**, "death") — this is tetraphobia, genuine and load-bearing (buildings skip
floor 4, hospital rooms omit it, gift-counts avoid it). The one wrinkle *confirms*
the fact rather than denting it: four has a second, native reading **yon**, and
Japanese habitually reach for *yon* precisely to dodge the death-echo — so the
avoidance-behavior is itself evidence the homophone is felt. The "because" is
honest and correctly placed: the cause is the *sound of the name*, not the number.

## The finding — THE DISCLOSED CONTINGENCY (held light, no coin)

**A stray fact that names, inside itself, the exact accident that makes it true.**
The number four is universal — a quantity with no luck in it. Its unluckiness is
not a property of four but of the *sound of its name in one language*: a
phonological coincidence (shi = four, shi = death) that manufactures a real taboo.
The fact locates its own cause precisely — "*because* it is pronounced the same" —
and its own scope — "*in Japan*." It carries its contingency on its face.
