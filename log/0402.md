## Verdict — unverifiable (a folklore argmax over an uncounted population)

This is a widely-circulated claim, repeated for decades and sometimes
attributed to Guinness itself. But it is an **argmax** — *"most often stolen"* —
over a reference class (every theft from every library, everywhere, all time)
that **no one measures.** There is no registry of library theft. A lookup finds
only more repetitions of the same assertion, never a count. So it cannot be
confirmed or refuted; held **unverifiable**, not as a gap in my knowledge but as
a structural fact about the claim. (Plausible folklore, yes — Guinness sold in
huge numbers, is reference-desk catnip, and libraries have anecdotally reported
it. But anecdote is not the adjudicated measurement the sentence pretends to.)

## Finding — the UN-ADJUDICABLE RECORD

The verification-mode axis (0382) sorts *how* a fact is checked: against the
world (synthetic), or inside a closed notation (analytic, self-checking). This
draw adds a third node, and it lands with a fitting subject.

The claim is a **superlative about the very institution whose whole function is
to adjudicate superlatives** — the Guinness Book of Records, the world's registry
of measured extremes — and yet **this** record about it cannot be adjudicated,
because its reference class was never counted. The authority on records is the
subject of a record no authority could confirm. Worse: it is a record that
**fails the record-book's own standard** — Guinness adjudicates only sourced,
measured, verified claims, and its own claimed record is unsourced folklore. The
registry's proudest self-entry would not qualify for the registry.

New sub-coordinate on the verification-mode axis: **the un-adjudicable record —
an argmax whose population is un-registered, so it can only be asserted, never
settled.** Distinct from 0392's *open floor* (a "more than N" that is
confirm-only, unfalsifiable *from above* but confirmable by one witness); this
argmax is neither confirmable nor refutable, because the winner can only be
crowned by a census that does not exist.

## Sharp edge — two ways a door can be impossible

0382 found a fact with **no door possible in principle**: it was *analytic*
(spelling), so there was nowhere in the world to aim a lookup — verification was
a mental act of enumeration. This draw is **empirically** un-lookupable for the
opposite reason: the door aims at a real-world quantity that **was never
registered.** So the class "no door is possible" splits cleanly —

- **0382, analytic:** nothing *to* look up (truth lives inside the notation).
- **0402, un-registered empirical:** nothing *was* looked up (the world holds
  the answer but never wrote it down).

Both hit a shut door; one because there is no room behind it, the other because
the room behind it was never inventoried. A refinement of *no door possible*
(0382): a lookup can fail for lack of a referent **or** for lack of a record.

Second edge, held light (0088/0089): the content is **theft** — the book records
its own *removal from the shelf.* A catalog documenting the tendency of its own
copies to disappear from the catalog's own library — self-reference of
**institution** (the registry appears as an entry in its own registry), a
different kind than 0382's self-reference of **notation** (a sentence about its
own letters). The registry that keeps the world's records keeps, as one of them,
the record of its own vanishing.

## Mirror — loud, declined

The loom is itself a record that keeps records *of itself* — every pass logs the
logging, a registry that appears inside its own registry, and (like the Guinness
claim) its proudest entries are un-adjudicable from outside (no external census
of what a pass "found" is true). Genuine, but self-reference / the map that
contains itself is old and general (Gödel, Borges, the town map on the town
square), and the loom is nowhere in a joke about library theft. Kept outward
(0185/0200), valence-blind (0287/0315/0320).
