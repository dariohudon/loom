## The finding — the false-share fact, refuted by its own arithmetic, carrying a true per-capita twin.

**Edge one — self-refutation by inversion, no world total needed.** Take the claim at
its word and run it backwards: if Russia's ~621,000 doctors are *one fifth* of all the
world's doctors, then the world holds only ~3.1 million doctors total. But that floor is
broken by almost any single large country — China's physician count alone is on the order
of the entire implied world total, and the US (~1M) and India (~1.3M) added to Russia
already blow past 3.1M (large-country counts recalled-not-checked, 0183; the *structure*
is robust to the exact figures). So the fact is **self-refuting**: multiplying out its own
"1 in 5" against the count the door confirmed produces a world too small to hold the
doctors we already know exist. This is the arithmetic-refutation move (kin to 0225's
self-verifying pane and 0265's "confirming the true half dissolves the false") — I did not
need to source a world total; the claim collapses against itself. Russia's real share is
roughly **1 in 20** (~5%), a top-few country by absolute count but nowhere near a fifth.

**Edge two — the true twin is a different MEASURE, and that's the new species.** The same
door that refutes the share-claim hands a genuine truth right beside it: **43.8 doctors per
10,000** places Russia among the world's *densest* in doctors per capita. So the
neighborhood contains a fact — but it is a **rate** (per-capita density), not a **share**
(fraction of all doctors). This distinguishes the pane sharply from the scope-elided fact
of **0241** (bees vs. snakes): there, restoring the omitted *frame* (US vs. global) flipped
the *same* quantity's truth value. Here the false claim and its true twin are not one
statement reframed — they are **two different statistics that sound alike**: Russia is dense
in doctors (true rate) ⇏ Russia holds most of the world's doctors (false share). The defect
is a **measure-swap** (density read as share), where 0241's was a **frame-swap**. Both hide
a reference-class error, but on orthogonal axes.

Discipline note (0235): I am *not* welding a false origin story onto the claim — I don't
assert the fabricator *did* confuse density for share; I only note that the true and false
statements are a rate and a share, easy to mistake for each other, and that the true one is
the survivor. Logical form (0196): a proportion/share claim, refutable arithmetically —
which is exactly how it fell.
