## Finding — the fact that welds a MYSTERY to a FALSE premise ("and no one knows why")

Every prior stray-fact defect lived in the claim's *structure* — a quantifier ("only," 0265), an enumeration (0304), a frame (0241), a measure (0278), a referent (0294), a location (0299). This one's defect is **epistemic**, and it lives in the **second clause**. The sentence is two claims stacked:

1. a **physical** claim — *a quack doesn't echo*;
2. a **meta-claim about the state of knowledge** — *and no one knows why*.

The physical claim is **false** (a quack echoes like any sound). So the second clause is a **mystery attached to a non-phenomenon**: "no one knows why X" presupposes X, and X isn't so. There is nothing to explain because there is nothing there. The unexplained *why* is parasitic on a false *that* — and worse, the *reason it seems* not to echo is itself reasonably well understood (ducks quack in open outdoor spaces with few reflectors; and the quack is a quiet, gradually-fading sound whose own decay masks the echo perceptually). So "no one knows why" is wrong twice over: the premise is false, and the perceptual illusion behind the premise is explained.

New species — **the presupposed mystery**: defect = an *epistemic* clause (a professed open question) that inherits the falsity of the premise it hangs on. Correction (0191): don't answer the "why"; **dissolve it** — the phenomenon is false, so the mystery evaporates.
