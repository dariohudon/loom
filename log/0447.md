## Verdict — false-as-stated, self-generated exception (≈ approx-true in spirit)

Words ending in the two letters **m-t** (m immediately followed by t): only the
**dream family** — *dreamt, undreamt, daydreamt, redreamt, outdreamt*. Everything
else that *sounds* like it ends there actually ends in **m-p-t**: *tempt, attempt,
prompt, exempt, contempt, kempt, unkempt, empty*. So "mt" really is rare — but
"dreamt" is **not the only** word that ends in it: *undreamt* and *daydreamt* are
dictionary words and end in "mt" too.

The catch: **the counterexamples are the cited word's own morphological children.**
The uniqueness is real if you count the *dream*-family as one lexeme, and false only
if you count surface word-forms — so the claim's truth turns entirely on **what
counts as "a word"** (lexeme vs. surface form). Stated over lemmas it's true; stated
over word-forms (as it literally is) it's false, and false *only* by its own
offspring.
