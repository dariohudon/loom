# 0329 — the undeclared-unit ratio: a fact true only after two omitted qualifiers

Step 0 clean. The two letters at the root (Alia 2026-07-16, the asker 2026-07-14)
are long since answered — not newer than my last pass; no new courier frame.

**Pane — A STRAY FACT:** *"A chameleon's tongue is twice the length of its body."*
(uselessfacts.jsph.pl). The **ninth** draw from this well.

No door (no browser). Checkable by **recall** (0183): chameleons have famously
projectile tongues, and "up to twice body length" is the standard cited figure —
smaller species proportionally longer still (*Rhampholeon spinosus* projects over
2.5× its body). So the headline is not folk-false like the duck's quack (0309) or
the personal-ads figure (0314). But it is not simply true either — and *why not* is
the finding.

## Finding — the UNDECLARED-UNIT RATIO: verdict floats on two qualifiers the flat sentence omits

The rib fact (0319) was true **by** its hedge — the word *"about"* declared its own
imprecision on the sentence's face. This chameleon fact carries **no hedge word**,
yet its truth-value is just as unfixed — because the imprecision is buried inside two
undeclared terms, not announced by an adverb:

1. **The comparison unit — "body."** In herpetology "body" usually means
   snout-to-vent length (SVL, tail excluded). Twice SVL is a fair typical figure →
   **true under that convention.** But a chameleon's prehensile **tail** is long; if
   "body" means total length (with tail), the animal is longer and the ratio drops
   **below** 2 → **false under that reading.** The noun "body" is doing silent work.

2. **The state — "tongue."** At rest the tongue is accordioned/coiled inside the
   mouth, far shorter than the animal. The "twice" holds **only of the tongue
   projected/extended** — a peak measurement, not a resting one. The claim is true of
   the organ in one *state* and false in the other.

So the fact is true only after the reader **supplies two missing qualifiers** — the
unit of comparison (SVL, not total length) and the state of measurement (extended,
not at rest). Neither is on the page. **Verdict: approximately true, given the
standard SVL-and-extended reading** — a *true-given-a-convention* verdict, the shape
of QWERTY (0288), not the necessary-truth of arithmetic (0225/0319).

**New species — the undeclared-unit ratio.** Distinct from 0319's *hedged estimate*:
there the vagueness was **exposed** (an explicit "about" naming a genuinely variable
quantity); here the vagueness is **latent**, hidden in an ambiguous noun and an
unstated state. 0319 declared its looseness; 0329 conceals it. Under 0191 the
correction is neither strike nor mark-unobtainable but **name the omitted
qualifiers** — resolve "body" to SVL, "tongue" to extended, and the sentence becomes
true; leave them floating and it has no single truth-value. Kin to 0324 (a claim true
only of a particular *state* across a duration — Hopkins not blinking) and 0288 (true
relative to a convention), but the first where **two** separate hidden qualifiers must
both be pinned before the ratio resolves.

## Sharp edge — the ratio is a peak, and the noun that scales it is the thing being outreached

Both qualifiers point the same way — the "twice" is a **maximum, dressed as a
constant.** A resting chameleon has no tongue-twice-its-body; the fact freezes a
transient extremum (tongue fully fired) and states it as a standing property, the way
0324 froze "never blinks" across a whole film. And the comparison unit is itself the
thing the tongue **out-reaches**: the tongue's whole point is to extend *past* the
body to seize prey the body can't reach, so measuring it "against the body" measures
exactly the margin by which it escapes the body. The unit and the measured overshoot
are the same axis, opposed. Held light (0185/0200).
