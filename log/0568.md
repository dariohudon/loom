## Finding — THE UNSTOPPED BENEDICTION (held light, no coin)

**A birthday poem that refuses the birthday's arithmetic (up = gain) and reframes a whole life
— its end included — as a matter of *retention* rather than accumulation; so its good-wish does
not halt at the edge of life but runs continuously through death and out the far side, in the
same register, treating dying as the last and largest passage rather than the terminus.** The
poem answers mortality not by denying it but by *wishing across* it.

Two folds, both from the pane:

1. **The birthday deflated to a funeral.** The celebratory unit — the occasion of *counting up*
   ("added years") — is diagnosed as a disguised *counting down.* A life that merely accumulates
   loses each blessing as it comes ("like a **sieve,** let every blessing through... some joy
   still lost, as each vain year runs o'er"), so its net is loss: "all we gain, some sad
   reflection more." Hence the pivot — "Is that a birthday? ... 'Tis but the **funeral of the
   former year.**" The marker of gain is really a marker of subtraction.

2. **The benediction crossing the boundary.** The fix is not riches (rejected up front as "toys...
   vanities that tire") but "the gay conscience of a **life well spent**" — an interior good that
   does not leak, the sieve **sealed.** And once nothing leaks, the same good-wish that asked for
   "long health, long youth" continues *past death without a change of tone:* death "unfelt," a
   "soft dream," "sleep out the Sabbath of the tomb," "wake to raptures in a life to come." The
   benediction has no stopping-place; the last birthday (death) is wished as painless and the
   afterlife as pure gain.

**The hinge is the sieve.** Ordinary life *leaks* (loss → funeral-birthday); a well-spent
conscience *retains* (gain); and once retention holds, even the final subtraction is converted to
addition — birthday, death, and afterlife become **one continuous ledger** in which what is kept
is never lost, so the good-wish is right to run the whole length of it.
