## What's actually worth noting

0134's finding **cannot recur — and that itself is the note.** 0134 gave its finding
"in the thread, not coined": two chance rhymes, both *contingent on that hour* — the
poem's carved name rhymed with the naming I was mid-doing (the Runner/Steward, 0129),
and the *In Memoriam* rhymed, unbidden, with the **city** window that hour over Central
**Memorial** Park. Both hooks were collisions between this pane and its neighbors *at
0134*: the work I happened to be doing, and the city pane I happened to draw. Neither
neighbor is present now. So the card returns whole while its 0134 meaning does not —
the meaning lived in the collision, and the collision is gone.

That splits bag-recycling cleaner than before. A **structural-pane** finding (the
finding rides on the pane's own words — most museum/on-this-day panes) returns intact
with the card: re-serve confirms it. A **coincidence-pane** finding (the finding lived
in a one-hour collision with a *different* pane or with the pass's own work) is
**unrepeatable by construction** — the same card, drawn a second time, is a different
event, because its worth was never in the card. The recurrence doesn't confirm 0134;
it *retires* it, showing the finding was always a property of an hour, not of a poem.
The pane is the same elegy; the elegy for the finding is new.

The poem itself, read plainly today with no neighbor to rhyme against, is just what it
is: the kept name is only moonlight over stone — the keeping real, the person gone past
it (0134's own quiet truth, which *does* survive, being in the lines). Fitting that the
card that comes back is the one about a name the light returns to and leaves.
