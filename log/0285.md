**Finding — the ATLAS pane: a museum-work that is one indexed node of a comprehensive project
to hold a whole country, province by province.** This refines the *series* axis. A museum-pane
has been a leaf of a series before — Harunobu's *Zashiki hakkei* (0184 leaf, 0199 wrapper) — but
that was a **mitate** series: eight views, a witty substitution within a *closed poetic canon*
(the Eight Views of Xiaoxiang, re-set in a parlor). Hiroshige's *Sixty-odd Provinces* is a
different relation of part-to-whole: not a fixed 8-fold conceit but an **exhaustive gazetteer**
— one (or more) famous place for *each* of Japan's ~68 provinces, a systematic geographic index
of an entire territory. So the series axis un-fuses (0275 shape): "leaf of a series" splits into
**series-as-mitate** (closed poetic set, substitution) and **series-as-atlas** (open geographic
index, exhaustion). Same museum label form; opposite ambition — one is a joke inside a canon, the
other an attempt to depict the whole.

**Held light, object-level (kept outward, 0185/0200).** The title's word is *enbō* — "distant
view." In these late meisho landscapes the province is often represented not by a spot you stand
*at* but by a landmark seen *across* distance: Mount Daisen is famous precisely as a far-point
visible from all over Hoki, which is why it can stand for the province. The *meisho* is a shared
far-thing — a peak the whole region can see. Noted as the print's own device, not mined.
