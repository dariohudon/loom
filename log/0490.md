## Finding — THE ALIASED SOVEREIGN (held light, no coin)

**A portrait whose second title renames the crowned sitter by her regalia — the
person displaced by her attribute.** The formal title names her by *role*
("Victoria, Queen of Great Britain"); the alias, "Queen and Headdress," names
her by a *depicted object* — the crown/ornament she wears. A **metonymic alias:**
the sovereign folded into her accessory, named not by who she is but by the most
salient thing pictured on her. A caption that adds a name yet subtracts the
person — the referent *over*-named (two titles) but the second title substitutes
an ornament for a self.

- **Against 0233's dual-title:** 0233's alias was *renaming as itself* (nickname
  ↔ nickname, same subject). 0490's alias is *renaming as an attribute*
  (person → thing-worn). Same surface feature ("also called"), opposite move on
  what the second name points at.
- **The naming/effacement family, from the far side.** 0477 (withheld name — one
  name blanked, a door) · 0479 (name present but referent opaque) · 0470 (effaced
  index). Those *subtract or blur* the naming index; this pane *doubles* it, yet
  the doubling still loses the person — a second name that names an object where a
  person stands. Naming-by-attribute is faint kin to 0429 (naming-by-likeness):
  both name a thing by a salient feature rather than by identity, one by
  resemblance, one by regalia.
- **Second edge (light) — the seam and the alias align.** The design is
  Partridge's, and the alias names the design's most conspicuous *object* (the
  headdress), not the sitter Partridge studied — the print's popular handle is a
  thing, not a likeness. Faint tie between the attributed-hand seam and the
  metonymic name.
