## Verdict — approx-true, structure survives, magnitudes don't

Two numbers and a date. Taken as *precise counts*, both terms are shaky (recall
unsworn):
- **25,000 citizens** — far too high for Spartiate *homoioi* (full citizens),
  who peaked near ~8,000–9,000 and by 400 BC were in steep demographic decline
  (*oliganthropia*), down toward a few thousand. The figure only works if
  "citizens" loosely swallows the *perioikoi* (free but non-Spartiate) — a
  silent redefinition of the counting unit.
- **500,000 slaves** — helots outnumbered the free, but most estimates sit
  nearer ~150,000–200,000; half a million is inflated.
- **"height of its power in 400 BC"** — actually defensible: Sparta's hegemony
  peaked just after 404 BC (the Peloponnesian War won).

So neither absolute count is reliable, and even the exact ratio (1:20) is at the
high end of the estimates. **But the thing the fact is *about* — that the
enslaved vastly, structurally outnumbered the free — is the true, famous,
textbook core of Sparta**, the fact that militarized the whole society around
fear of a helot revolt. The truth survives at the level of the **relation's
direction and extremity**; it is lost in the magnitudes and even in the precise
proportion.

## Finding — THE STANDING INEQUALITY (held light, no coin)

**A fact whose two absolute figures are both inflated, yet the inequality they
enact is the real, famous truth — truth carried by the *direction* of the
relation, not by either term or their exact size.** Inflate both counts and the
20:1 shock still lands; the datum is wrong in its numbers and right in its
*shape*.

- **Against 0417 (THE WISHFUL FALSE), a clean inverse:** 0417 was magnitude-right
  / sign-wrong (a true near-zero pushed across zero into falsehood). Here it is
  **direction-right / magnitude-wrong** — the inequality points the correct way,
  only the sizes are off. Same fault-line (which layer of a claim carries the
  truth), opposite face: 0417 loses the sign and keeps the size; this loses the
  size and keeps the sign.
- **Against 0427 (THE FLATTERED FIGURE), one axis over:** 0427 inflated a *single*
  number *toward a better story* (distortion in the service of narrative). Here
  *both* numbers inflate but in a way that **preserves the point** — inflation
  without distortion of what's being claimed. The exaggeration is faithful to the
  structure it exaggerates.
- **Second edge, held light — the two peaks that diverged.** The sentence pins
  peak *power* and peak *population* to one date. For most polities those move
  together; for Sparta they came apart — its power peaked (~404–400 BC) precisely
  as its *citizen* body collapsed. So "25,000 citizens at the height of power" is
  self-undermining in a way the sentence can't see: Sparta was never more powerful
  and never had fewer full citizens than in these same years. A faint kin of
  0407's dateless-snapshot (a date given, but the variable it's attached to was
  moving the *opposite* way).
