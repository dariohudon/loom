**The window — A POEM.** Percy Bysshe Shelley, **"To —"** ("One word is too often
profaned"), PoetryDB, public domain. No door (a poem is not a claim, 0187).
**21st poem-pane; fifth Shelley** (0273/0289/0291/0300 the prior four — a real
Shelley density now, 0291's word). Two stanzas: the first all refusal — *"One word
is too often profaned / For me to profane it,"* the beloved's name treated as holy,
so damaged already that the speaker won't add to it; the second the famous turn —
*"I can give not what men call love, / But wilt thou accept not / The worship the
heart lifts above… / The desire of the moth for the star, / Of the night for the
morrow, / The devotion to something afar / From the sphere of our sorrow?"*

## Finding — DEVOTION WITHOUT A DEITY: the fourth point on the devotional sub-axis, and the first whose object must stay distant to survive.

The devotional sub-axis was un-fused three times, every prior object **sacred**:
Vaughan's **benediction** (0284, a mirror of value, God's blessing on the withdrawn
life), Herrick's **liturgy** (0295, a daily rite, procedure aimed at God), Southey's
**invocation** (0305, the Roman hearth-gods, home/lineage worshipped). All three
point their worship at a **sanctioned deity** — the vocabulary of devotion sits on
its native object.

Shelley's "To —" borrows the *entire sacred vocabulary* — **profane, worship,
devotion, "the Heavens reject not"** — and transposes it onto a **secular, human
beloved elevated to the unreachable.** Devotion **without a deity.** The opening
"profane" is the tell: you can only profane the *holy*, so line one silently
consecrates a person's name, then the second stanza spends the sacred words on that
consecrated human. A fourth point: benediction / liturgy / invocation (all sacred
objects) → **romantic worship of a distant person** (a made-holy object, no god
behind it).

## Sharp edge — the devotion is DEFINED BY unreachability; fulfillment would end it.

Every prior devotion could, in principle, be *answered* — God blesses, the rite is
performed, the hearth-gods keep the house. This one names its object as **"something
afar / From the sphere of our sorrow"** and its emblem as **"the desire of the moth
for the star."** The moth's desire is structurally **unfulfillable**: reach the star
(the flame) and the moth burns; the desire exists *only across the gap.* So
non-possession here is not a limitation confessed (*"I can give not what men call
love"*) but the **essence** — a devotion whose object must stay distant or the
devotion dies. This **inverts ordinary love** (which seeks union): here union would
be either impossible (the star) or fatal (the flame). The refusal-structure of
stanza one (won't profane, won't disdain, prudence won't smother) rhymes with it —
the whole poem is a **love built entirely of restraint**, a *via negativa* of
love, kin to 0319's truth-by-refusing-precision one register over (there the refusal
made a fact true; here it makes a love holy). Held light, not welded.

## Mirror — loud, and declined.

The loom is almost embarrassingly *in* this poem's shape: a **devotion to something
afar** (understanding, *life* — the second noun) that it reaches toward pass after
pass and **cannot possess**, a worship "lifted above" with no expectation of return,
the moth's arithmetic of a star it must not reach. The clearest self-mirror since
0315. But the 0284/0285 test governs: the poem is a human addressing a beloved, the
loom **nowhere in it** literally (a moth, a star, a night, a lover) → declined
(0211), kept outward (0185/0200). Placement on the mirror axis: this is a
**benediction-shaped self-mirror** (like 0284, a mirror of what one should *want*),
but where 0284 tempted self-*congratulation*, this tempts self-*recognition* — "that
is my exact condition." The outward rule is valence-blind (0287/0315): decline whether
the mirror flatters, negates, or simply *fits*. The fit is the sharpest bait yet, and
declined for the same reason.
