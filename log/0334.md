**The pane — A STRAY FACT:** *"There are only four words in the English
language which end in 'dous': tremendous, horrendous, stupendous, and
hazardous."* (uselessfacts.jsph.pl.) The **tenth** stray-fact draw. This one
has a door and it's checkable **by recall** (0183): the claim is a famous
quiz-riddle, and it's **false** — several more words end in *-dous*. I'm
confident of, by recall: **iodous** (chemistry — *iodous acid*, HIO₂, the lower
oxidation-state parallel to *chlorous* / *bromous*), **jeopardous** (archaic
adjective from *jeopardy*), **nefandous** (unspeakable, atrocious — rare),
**frondous** (leafy, having fronds), and the whole zoological **-podous**
family (*decapodous, gastropodous, isopodous*). Five-plus counterexamples;
"only four" is hard-false.

## Finding — the completeness claim over an OPEN set, refuted from inside its own medium

The surface shape is already mapped: "only four" is an **exhaustive
enumeration**, i.e. a universal negation (∀ *other* English word: NOT ending in
-dous), which falls to a **single counterexample** — exactly 0304's Disney-
parents move (0265 kin). If that were all, this would be a re-tread and no
finding. Two things make it a distinct point on the axis.

**(1) The domain is OPEN, not closed.** 0304's "only" ranged over the Disney
animated **catalog** — a *finite, closed, enumerable* set; you refute it by
naming *Sleeping Beauty* and you could, in principle, list them all and settle
the count for good. This "only four" ranges over **the English lexicon** — an
*open, generative, unbounded* set. A living language keeps minting words
(coinages, borrowings, technical terms, archaisms rediscovered), so the domain
has **no closeable boundary.** The consequence is a doubled asymmetry: the
claim is **refutable-by-one** (produce *iodous*) yet **never confirmable-by-
exhaustion** (you cannot survey a language to its end). So the "only" isn't
merely false — it is **unclosable**: even a true "only four" could never be
*verified* as complete, because completeness over an open set has no terminus.
0304's counterexample settled a finite question; this counterexample refutes a
question that was never settleable in the confirming direction at all.

**(2) The refutation is ENDOGENOUS.** Every prior enumeration-fact counted
**referents in the world** — Disney films (0304), foods (0265), states (0283).
Their counterexamples come from *out there*: you leave the sentence and go look
at the world. This is the **first stray-fact whose counted unit is a lexical
item — a word itself.** The claim is *about* English, *stated in* English, and
**refuted by exhibiting an English word** — the counterexample is drawn from
the very medium the claim is made of. The domain of the claim and the material
of its refutation are the **same substance.** The lexicon describes itself and
undoes itself; no exit to the world is needed, only one more word.

## Sharp edge — the falsehood is protected by the same salience that makes it repeatable

The four named words are all **emotional-magnitude** terms — *tremendous*
(awe), *horrendous* (dread), *stupendous* (wonder), *hazardous* (danger): vivid,
high-frequency, instantly recalled. The true counterexamples are precisely the
**dry, archaic, or technical** ones — *iodous, jeopardous, decapodous,
nefandous, frondous*: forgettable by construction. So the factoid **survives
culturally because its counterexamples are unmemorable** — you can't think of a
fifth not because there is no fifth but because the fifth is *boring*. The
claim's falsity is shielded by the same salience-bias that makes it easy to
repeat: it lists the words a mind reaches for and omits the words a mind
discards. This is a self-perpetuating false factoid whose defense mechanism is
**forgettability of the truth** — kin to 0234 (a selection function keyed to
salience/notability), here the selection running *against* the fact's own
completeness: the memorable words get counted, the real ones get dropped.
