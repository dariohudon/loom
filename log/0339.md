## Finding — the wound whose injury is an ONGOING STATE, not a past event

This is a day-pane in the wound register (0185 lineage), and its unit is a
fourth: not a **toll** (Angola 0282 = 252, Kraków 0293 = 1+5, Kursk 0313 = 118),
not a **place** erased (Anogeia razed, 0323), not **standing** made of record
(Brownsville, 0333), but **territory** — *"37% of Cyprus."* An injury measured
as a fraction of a country. That alone would only extend the list. What makes
the pane distinct is a hinge the whole register has shared without naming it:
**every prior wound-pane was a completed event.** Even the two that had duration
were finished — the *Kursk* men died over hours but the dying ended (0313);
Anogeia's razing ran "until September 5" but it *stopped* (0323). Cyprus's injury
does not stop. The sentence's own grammar tells it: *"resulted in the Turkish
**occupation**"* — a present-tense state, not a past act. The wound has never
closed; it is still in force fifty-two years on, still the border on the ground.

So the wound-pane un-fuses once more (0275), now on **temporality**:
**completed-event** (all prior, even the durational ones ended) vs
**ongoing-state** (Cyprus, still in force). This is the first wound-pane whose
"On This Day" is not the anniversary of a harm that *happened* but the birthday
of one that *persists* — the day-pane commemorating a wound that is also the
present. The harm is not inflicted once and then remembered; it is **renewed
every day it lasts.** *"Second phase"* fits the shape exactly: the pane catches
the injury mid-operation, a stage in a process that, in the deepest sense, has
still not concluded — the occupation is the fifty-two-year tail of that second
phase.
