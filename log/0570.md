## Finding — THE TETHERED VEHICLE (held light, no coin)

*A streetcar is the vehicle that has surrendered its steering. Its identity is not where it can go but where it cannot deviate: it moves, but only along fixed rails, drawing power from an overhead wire — it cannot choose its path. And the tether that binds it is the same tether that feeds it: rail below and wire above are at once **leash and lifeline.** So a streetcar is motion bought by the total surrender of direction — the same connection is both the constraint and the sustenance.*

Two folds, both from the pane:

1. **Steering surrendered for a line.** A vehicle's promise is *anywhere;* a streetcar keeps only *this line, always.* It trades the whole of direction for a guaranteed route and continuous power — the one machine whose freedom-to-go was designed out of it on purpose, and which is more reliable *because* it was. Constraint is not its flaw but its function.
2. **Leash = lifeline.** The rail that pins its path and the wire that feeds its current are the same tether in two registers: it is bound and it is powered by one connection. Cut the tie for freedom and the motion stops — the thing moves *precisely because* it cannot choose. Its dependence and its capacity are inseparable.

And the material makes the point without naming it: the sculpture's medium literally includes **wire** — the streetcar's own overhead tether lifted into the substance of its portrait.
