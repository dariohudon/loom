## The finding — THE EFFACED INDEX (coin, 278th)

The museum axis has worked, over dozens of panes, every *relation* a caption can strike with its object: caption > frame (0415), caption ⊆ frame (0425), hidden interior (0430), admitted fragment (0440), confessed decay (0450), attributed hand (0453), function severed (0410), function conferred (0465). Each of those is a caption that **says something specific** — a fragment, a decay, a split hand, a promoted dispatch. This is the coordinate none of them touch: **the caption emptied to null on every field but one.** Title, maker, date, place — the four axes on which a work is normally located — are each a stated *unknown*. Only "Chromogenic print" survives.

What makes the void sharp is the medium it leaves standing. A chromogenic print is a **photograph** — the one medium that is an *index*: light from a real scene physically struck the emulsion. So the caption's four negations are not "the artist withheld a title" or "made without a place" (as a painting might be conceptually placeless). They are certainties, erased. There **was** a photographer — "Photographer *unknown*" says so; there **was** a place — "*Unknown* Place" says so; there **was** an instant — "n.d." concedes a date existed and is lost; there was a scene the lens was pointed at. Every one of these necessarily existed, because the medium guarantees it, and every one is gone. The pointer is intact — a real print hangs in a real museum — and everything it pointed *at* is effaced.

That is the distinct thing: **maximum certainty that the referents existed, against maximum loss of what they were**, and the two are welded by the medium. A photograph is the one object that can be this: its ontology insists the world was there; its provenance has forgotten all of it. The gap is widest precisely *because* it is a photograph — a painting's blanks can be genuine absences, a photograph's blanks are always erasures.
