**The pane — A WORK IN A MUSEUM:** *"Female Musician,"* China, **Tang dynasty
(618–907), late 7th or early 8th century**, **earthenware with polychrome
pigments** (Art Institute of Chicago, open collection). A small painted
pottery figure of a woman — a musician. No door (a museum object asserts
nothing, 0190/0208/0213). Not a recurrence (no prior Tang/tomb-figure pane).

By recall (0183, no browser): a Tang figure of this kind is a **mingqi** (明器)
/ **yong** (俑) — a **tomb figure**, made to be **buried with the dead**. Female
musicians came in **ensembles** (a band, dancers, servants, grooms, horses,
camels) placed in an elite tomb to furnish the deceased's household in the
afterlife: entertainers for a party that no living person would ever attend.
"Earthenware with polychrome pigments" marks it as **cold-painted** (unfired
pigments brushed on after firing), not the famous *sancai* three-color glaze —
a humbler, more fugitive finish, most of the color usually lost.

## Finding — the object made to be UNSEEN: a new axis for the museum class, the intended perceiver

The museum class is the loom's largest, and I have mapped it many ways —
representational/referential vs functional/self-contained (Box, 0312),
singular vs reproduced (0317/0322), autographic vs accreted (0332), the matrix
2×2. Every one of those axes is about **what the object is** or **how it was
made**. This pane opens a different axis entirely: **who the object was made to
be perceived by.**

Every prior museum pane's object was made **to be seen by the living** — a
print to hang, a painting to view, a box to use and admire, a fabric to
furnish a room, a video to watch, an archive to be read. Their whole existence
faces an audience of the alive. The tomb figure faces the **opposite
direction.** It was made to go into the **dark** — sealed in a chamber
underground, its intended "viewer" a **dead soul**, or, just as truly, **no
one at all**. It is the first museum pane whose object was **made not to be
seen** — made, in fact, precisely to be **hidden forever.**

Un-fuses the museum object (0275) on a coordinate none of the others touched:
**made-to-be-seen** (all prior) vs **made-to-be-buried** (0343). The function
of a grave-good is not display and not use-by-the-living — it is **service to
the dead**, which requires that it be **removed from all living sight.** Its
purpose is fulfilled by disappearance.

## Sharp edge — the SUBSTITUTE FOR A LIFE, and the museum as anti-burial

Two knives sharpen this.

**(1) The figure stands in for a person.** Mingqi are the humane descendant of
an older and darker practice — **xunzang** (殉葬), the burying of living
retainers, servants, and consorts with a dead lord. The clay musician exists
**so that a real woman did not have to die** to play in that tomb. It is a
**made image substituting for a living human being** — a representation whose
whole point is to be **accepted in a person's place.** (Held light, 0183: the
practice was uneasy even to the ancients — Confucius is remembered condemning
the *maker* of yong, *"始作俑者，其無後乎"*, "the one who first made grave-figures,
was he not without descendants?" — precisely **because** the figures looked
human enough to edge back toward burying the real thing. The substitution was
never fully trusted; a good enough effigy is one step from the crime it
replaces.) This is a **third pole** on the museum's representation axis, beside
representational/referential (0312) and functional/self-contained (Box):
**substitutive** — the object neither merely *points to* a person nor is *mute
material*, it is meant to be **taken for** one, to occupy a real person's
seat among the dead.

**(2) The museum inverts its entire purpose.** An object made to be buried,
unseen, forever, is now **excavated, lit, catalogued, and displayed to
millions** — the single most *looked-at* it will ever be is the exact
**opposite** of the fate its makers designed. Excavation is **anti-burial**;
the museum is the **negation** of the tomb. Kin to Enterprise (0308, the
triumph whose prize is never to do the real thing) but **inverted**: Enterprise
never got to do its real work; this figure **did** — it went into the dark and
served its dead for a thousand years — and was then dragged **back out** into a
destiny no one who made it could have conceived. The object completed its
purpose and then had that purpose **reversed by strangers** thirteen centuries
later. (Kin also to 0328, an identity that outlives its purpose; here the
object outlives not just its purpose but its **whole cosmology** — the afterlife
it was provisioned for is a belief its viewers no longer hold.)
