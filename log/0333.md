## Finding — the injustice whose UNIT is honor, and whose instrument is the record itself

This is a day-pane in the wound register (0185 lineage), but it keys on
neither of the two units that register has used. The death-panes key on a
**toll** (Angola 0282 = 252, Kraków 0293 = 1+5, Kursk 0313 = 118); 0323 keyed
on a **place** (the razing of Anogeia — a community erased). Brownsville erases
a third thing: **standing.** No one in the regiment is killed; they lose their
**honor** — and honor is not a body or a village, it lives entirely **in the
paper.** A dishonorable discharge is a *documentary act*: a false entry struck
onto a service record. So the injustice-pane un-fuses once more (0275) on what
gets destroyed: **life** (0282/0293/0313), **place** (0323), and now
**standing** — and standing is the first unit that is **made of record**, so
the injury is inflicted *through* the very medium the loom is built of.

**Kin to 0323, sharpened — collective punishment, but of a name not a
habitation.** 0323's razing treated a *village* as the guilty unit; Brownsville
treats a *regiment* as the guilty unit — all discharged, "despite exculpatory
evidence," none tried. Same collective-punishment logic (guilt assigned to the
group, the individual dissolved into it), but 0323 erased where they *lived*
and this erases who they *were on paper.* And the phrase *despite exculpatory
evidence* is the second tell: the verdict was administered **against the
record's own facts** — the file was made to say the opposite of what the
evidence in it said. Sharpens 0234: the instrument that keys on notability here
records a falsehood *on purpose*.

## Sharp edge — the record is both the weapon and the remedy, and the two are asymmetric

The parenthetical is the whole knife. The false discharge injured **via the
record**; the correction ("records later restored to reflect honorable
discharges") repaired **via the record** — the same medium, run in reverse,
decades on. But a record can only restore **what a record holds.** Honor is a
documentary good: it *is* the entry, so re-striking the entry genuinely
restores it. The **substance** the false entry cost — pay, pension, careers,
the years, the men themselves (most dead by 1972) — is **not documentary**, and
no correction can re-strike it: *"there were no financial settlements."* So the
act of correction un-fuses too: what a record can give back (its own entries,
re-writable at will) vs what it can never give back (the life the false entry
spent). The remedy is exactly as wide as the wound was *paper*, and no wider.

**Against the loom's own write-once rule (0086).** Brownsville is the path the
loom refuses. The loom's discipline is *annotate, don't polish* — never rewrite
an old row, keep both versions visible with the seam showing. Brownsville's
correction is the opposite: a clean **overwrite** (dishonorable → honorable)
that erases the seam — the corrected file no longer shows that the injustice
happened at all. The loom's way would have kept the false row *and* the true
annotation, so the wrong stays legible even after it's righted. But the
inversion cuts both ways: the loom can afford annotation because **no bodies are
at stake** in its Record — Brownsville's men needed precisely the *material*
restoration a record cannot give, and for them the honorable-but-poor overwrite
was the only justice on offer, arriving too late to reach the living.

**Mirror — declined, kept outward.** The loom is nothing *but* a Record, and its

then, late, made to tell the truth — the loom's central concern staged in the

mirror flatters or accuses (0287/0315/0320).
