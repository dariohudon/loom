## Verdict — true only under an omitted qualifier

The claim holds for **dried** fly meal, not for the thing named. Dried adult
houseflies run ~50–60% protein by dry mass; beef is ~20% by wet mass. But a
**fresh** housefly is ~70%+ water — so *a pound of houseflies* as literally
stated (whole, hydrated) has roughly beef-comparable protein or **less.** The
comparison only comes out the flies' way if you dry them first (or compare
dry-weight to dry-weight). The load-bearing word — **"dried"** — is exactly the
word left out. With it the fact is true; without it, false or at best a coin
toss. So: not a wrong number. A wrong **frame.**

## Finding — THE UNLIKE POUND (held light, no coin)

*A comparison in matched units whose two terms are in unmatched states — the
equal unit (a pound vs. a pound) laundering an unequal comparison.*

"A pound of X vs. a pound of Y" is the **grammar of a controlled comparison:**
same unit, same quantity, so *only the substance differs* — that is the whole
rhetorical force of pound-for-pound. Here the control is fake. One term is
implicitly **concentrated** (dried, water driven off), the other **whole**
(beef, water in). The parallel frame *guarantees* a fairness the content
**violates:** the matched unit does the deceiving, not the datum inside it. The
fraud is that the two pounds are not weighing the same kind of thing.

**New stray-fact fault-family — the comparison frame / mismatched measure.**
Not content-wrong (the dry-weight fact is real), not un-adjudicable (both terms
are perfectly countable) — but **frame-deceptive:** a *true* comparison whose
framing implies a commensurability the terms lack.

### Edge (light) — the pound is honest, the states are not

Nothing about "a pound" is false; both weighings would come out to sixteen
ounces. The deception needs no false measurement at all — it rides entirely on
the reader's assumption that two things weighed on the same scale are being
*compared* on the same scale. The unit is the alibi.
