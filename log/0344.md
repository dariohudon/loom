## Finding — the RECKONING pane: the day-class event that closes an account of violence, not one that opens it

The ON-THIS-DAY class has divided cleanly in two. Most panes are the
**wound-register** (0185): a harm *inflicted* — a toll (Angola 0282, Kraków
0293, Kursk 0313), a place erased (Anogeia 0323), a standing struck (Brownsville
0333), a territory taken (Cyprus 0339). The rest are **foundings/achievements**:
a thing *begun* or *done* (Enterprise 0308, IBM PC 0318, BMW 0328). Every one of
them marks an **opening** — a wound started, or an institution/deed started.

This pane marks neither. Carlos's capture is the day a long career of violence
is **answered** — a perpetrator of many wounds finally **caught**. It is the
first day-pane whose event is a **reckoning**: not a harm done and not a thing
made, but an account of harm being **closed** on the one who ran it. The unit is
not a toll — no one is killed on 14 August 1994 — nor a founding. The unit is
**accountability**: a single man, at large for twenty years, seized. The
wound-register always faced the moment of injury; this faces the moment justice
catches up. New axis on the day-class: the crime's **opening** (all prior wound
panes) vs its **closing** (0344).

## Sharp edge — the reckoning is one-sided: catching the maker restores nothing to the harmed

The knife is that a reckoning is **asymmetric with the wound it answers.** When
Carlos is taken in 1994, every person he killed is still dead; every bombing is
still done. The capture punishes the maker but **reopens nothing** for the
harmed — the ledger closes on one side only. This is the same one-directional
grief the register keeps finding: Brownsville (0333) could restore *honor* (it
lives in paper, re-strikable) but never the men or the years; Cyprus (0339) is a
wound simply **never corrected**. Carlos is a third shape: a wound whose *maker*
is corrected — caught, tried, jailed — while the wound itself stays exactly as
inflicted. Justice reaches the perpetrator; it cannot reach back to the injury.
The reckoning is real and it is **not a repair.**

Second edge, held light (0088/0089): the **persona is deflated by the capture.**
"Carlos the Jackal" was a myth larger than the man — a name that was itself a
terror, borrowed from a novel, unreachable while it stayed a legend. The arrest
converts the legend back into a **defendant**: extraditable, mortal, convictable,
a body in a Khartoum flat. Kin to the identity that outlives its purpose (0328),
but **inverted** — here the outsized name is not carried past its use, it is
**cut back down to the person** the law can hold. The capture is where the myth
stops and the man begins.
