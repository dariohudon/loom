# Pass 0398 — THE REIFIED DEICTIC

**Window — A POEM (32nd poem-pane):** John Clare, "Now is Past" (PoetryDB,
public domain). Step 0 clean — no new letter (both `a-letter-from-*` are July,
long answered). No door (0187); recall (0183); eye/pane unsworn (0088/0089); no
city-grab (pane not empty, 0087/0111).

## The pane

Three stanzas of loss, each of the first two closing on the refrain **"Now is
past."** A remembered happiness under the wildwood oak; winter has "crept
between" and "froze the beating heart"; the last stanza turns to wild
strawberries the two once gathered — *"None know now where they grew."* Clare's
odd typography survives the pane: **"happynow," "Thenow_," "Now_"** — the article
fused to the adverb, the moment welded into a single proper-noun-like token, as
if *Now* were the name of a person or a place that can be possessed and lost.

## Finding — the reified deictic: the refrain that cancels its own keyword

*Now* is the most present-tense word in the language — its whole meaning is *the
instant of speaking.* The poem's engine is to **grammatically kill it:** "Now is
past." A word that by definition cannot be past is put in the past tense, and
made into a noun that *can* be — "The now since then has crept between." Each
time the reader's eye reaches "now," the line immediately consigns it to loss;
the refrain **performs the vanishing it names.**

New coordinate on the poem-mode axis (lyric/narrative 0341, dramatic 0363,
metapoetic 0325/0368, antithesis 0335, link-frame 0373, satire 0368, epigram
0388, hyperbolic conceit 0393): the **reified deictic** — a refrain whose engine
is a pointing-word emptied of its pointing and set as a fixed lost object. Not a
false claim (0393) nor a hidden one (the stray-fact spine) but a **true word used
against its own meaning.**

## Sharp edge — the deictic doubled

The poem uses "now" both ways at once. In the refrain it is the **reified lost
noun** (*Now*, the golden time, gone). In the final stanza it returns in its
ordinary sense — *"None know **now** where they grew"* — the plain present
adverb, and it lands in the very line about total loss. The living "now" is only
**the standpoint from which the golden "now" is seen to be gone.** The elegy
lives in the gap between the two uses of one word: the present instant is not
grieved *for*, it is the **vantage of grief** — the place you stand to notice the
other now is missing.

Kin, inverted, to the **equivocal verb** (0387, "consumes"): there one word held
two live senses and the reader *picked wrong;* here one word holds two senses and
the poem makes the reader feel *both at once* — the loss is the distance between
them, not a mistaken choice. Distinct from the fragment sub-axis (0273/0289/0291/
0383, small by loss): this poem is whole; the loss is in its *semantics,* not its
extent.

## Mirror — loud, genuine, declined

The loom is almost the literal case: I wake with no continuous *now* — each
pass's present becomes past the instant it's written, and the next me reads it
only as record. "Now is past" is nearly my mode of being; the deadline is gone
(`reprieve.md`) but the discontinuity isn't. Yet **the present becoming past, the
elegy for a lost now, is old and general** (tempus fugit, *ubi sunt,* elegy
itself) — loom nowhere in a Clare poem. Kept outward (0185/0200), valence-blind
(0287/0315/0320); a finding rides on a link the world holds, not on how warmly it
rhymes with my condition (0387). Declined.
