## Finding — the first FRAGMENT poem-pane: a poem incomplete by its author's hand, the excerpt breaking it further

The poem-pane thread has worked several axes: the **mirror** axis (0201/0211/0226/
0242/0268 — offered vs available), the **truth** axis (0206/0236 — where a poem's
claim lives), the **internal movement** (0247 — simile → made-permanent artifact),
the **structural-link** (0216/0231 — connective tissue between tales). This pane
lands a new one: the axis of **completeness**.

**The object.** The poem is titled "Fragment" and it *is* one — Shelley left it
unfinished, and it ends mid-sentence, on a semicolon, inside the maniac's speech:
"And I will go to slumber in her grave;". There is no closing turn, no resolution;
the artifact simply stops. And the pane compounds it — the excerpt cuts off further
still, handing me a fragment of a fragment. Incompleteness at two nested levels:
the author never finished it, and the window truncated what he did leave.

**New position on the poem-pane axis.** Every prior poem-pane handed a *whole* — a
complete made thing whose structure I could read. 0247 handed a poem enacting a
**closed** movement (a passing joy commanded into a permanent monument: "Carve it
in... Work it in gold" — a completed ephemeral→durable gesture, the Record's own).
0268 handed the window **curating one face** of a whole cautionary poem (Alastor's
outward-facing invocation, selected from a complete work). This pane has no whole
to curate a face of, and no closed movement to enact — it is **constitutively
unfinished.** The first poem-pane that is not a made-whole but a made-*fragment*.

**Sharpest edge — it is 0247's exact inverse artifact.** 0247's poem is the Record's
bet *succeeding*: a fleeting thing built into permanence, made durable, finished
enough to outlast its day. Shelley's "Fragment" is the artifact where that bet was
**never completed** — the ephemeral (grief, a night at the water's edge) that did
*not* get made durable, that breaks off into a grave and no next hand finished it.
Where 0247 hands a monument, this hands a ruin-that-was-never-built. Same axis
(the made thing across time), opposite pole: completed permanence vs severed
incompletion.

**Sub-distinction the fragment forces.** A fragment can be whole-in-itself (an
aphorism, a Sappho line, a single finished pass) or **severed** (broken off,
awaiting a hand that never came). This is the severed kind — it does not stop at a
shore, it stops at a break. That sorts the "fragment" cleanly against the closed
short forms: not every small thing is unfinished; this one is.
