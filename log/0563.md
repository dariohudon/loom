**Finding — THE OWED FAULT (held light, no coin): a speaker who narrates his
entire moral condition — his follies, his broken peace, his "early faults," his
lost purity, even his coming promiscuity — as *caused wholly by the beloved*,
retaining no authorship of his own conduct; and who casts this in the grammar of
gratitude ("To thee, these early faults I owe"), so the debtor's idiom of thanks
carries the content of an accusation.** Grievance as a *total transfer of agency*:
the beloved is pure cause, the speaker pure effect, and even his sins are deeded
to her account.

**Three folds in one lyric.**
1. **The gratitude-grammar carrying blame.** *"To thee, these early faults I
   owe."* "I owe you ——" is the debtor's language of *thanks*; you expect it to
   deliver joys, growth, a gift received. Here it delivers **faults** — he thanks
   her, in the exact idiom of gratitude, for making him bad. Form says *thanks*,
   cargo says *you ruined me.* The frame and its content point opposite ways, and
   the bitterness is *heard through* the courtesy.
2. **The renounced authorship.** Every stratum of his ruin is charged to her: the
   follies "had not, then, been mine" but for her; his "peace... not been broken"
   but for her; his soul "like thine, was pure" until "thy vows no more endure."
   Nothing he is remains his own doing. He keeps no residue of agency — the self
   is narrated as a *thing that happened to him,* authored elsewhere.
3. **The dispersal, also deeded.** *"what it sought in thee alone, / Attempts,
   alas! to find in many."* Fidelity, denied its one object, becomes infidelity —
   the singular love scatters into promiscuity — and that too is laid to her
   charge ("since thy angel form is gone"). Even the coming betrayals are her
   fault, pre-billed to the one who left. So too the one apparent virtue: *"let my
   Rival smile in joy... I cannot hate him. / For thy dear sake"* — his
   forbearance toward the man who supplanted him is **not his own magnanimity but
   her residue,** love routed *past* its natural enemy and landing on mercy he
   does not author either. The whole ledger — sins and mercies alike — is signed
   in her name.

**Sharpest vs 0559 (THE ENACTING PROLOGUE) — form/content, aligned vs. opposed.**
0559 a frame whose *form performs its content* (a prologue on "beholding
transforms the beholder" that transforms its reader — thanks-shaped thanks, the
frame a working sample of its own goods) / 0563 a frame whose *form belies its
content* (gratitude-shaped accusation — "I owe you" delivering faults, the frame
the enemy of its cargo) — **self-demonstrating frame vs. self-contradicting
frame.** Both are the *form doing work on the content;* they split by whether the
work confirms or inverts.

**Vs 0553 (THE UNLIKE POUND) — deceptive frame vs. avowed frame.** 0553 a matched
unit laundering unmatched terms, a *deception* (the reader is meant not to notice
the frame lies) / 0563 the gratitude-idiom carrying blame is **avowed** — no one
is fooled, the accusation is *supposed* to be heard through the courtesy, the
irony is the poem's whole expressive point. **Frame-that-hides vs.
frame-that-shows** (kin to 0559's manufactured-and-avowed line).

**Vs 0547 (THE SELF-CONFERRED RANK) — authorship grabbed vs. authorship shed.**
0547 the self *authors its own elevation* (grants itself a status no third party
gave — agency **claimed** where it isn't owed) / 0563 the self *disowns its own
conduct* (assigns its faults to another — agency **renounced** where it *is*
owed). Both mis-place the authorship of a self: one grabs credit it hasn't earned,
the other sheds fault it has. **Self-conferred vs. self-disowned** — the two
directions a self can lie about who authored it.

**Edge (light) — the address survives its own verdict.** He calls her "deceitful
Maid" in the last line yet keeps the reverent register throughout ("thy angel
form," "For thy dear sake") — the condemnation and the devotion held in one
breath, neither cancelling the other; the transfer of agency *requires* her to
stay exalted (only a cause that great could author a ruin this total).
