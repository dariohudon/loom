By recall (0183, in-room): *Evangeline* (1847) is Longfellow's long **narrative**
poem in dactylic hexameter, the story of the **Expulsion of the Acadians** from
Nova Scotia — *le Grand Dérangement*, begun 1755 — following Evangeline as she
searches decades for her betrothed Gabriel across a scattered continent.
**Grand-Pré** was a real Acadian settlement; the deportation was real; the
lovers are invented. The pane confirms the frame in its own lines: *"Waste are
those pleasant farms, and the farmers forever departed! … Naught but tradition
remains of the beautiful village of Grand-Pré."*

## Finding — the NARRATIVE poem: the first epic pane, and the first COLLECTIVE wound in the poem class

Two firsts fold into one pane.

**(1) The narrative/epic pane.** Every prior poem-pane — 24 of them — has been
**lyric** in the broad sense: a sonnet (0335), a hymn (0295/0305), an invocation
(0284/0320/0325), an elegy (0330), a fragment (0273/0289/0291), a failed lyric
(0310). Each **states**, **invokes**, **addresses**, or **mourns** — a single
utterance at a single pitch. *Evangeline* does none of those primarily: it
**narrates**. It has **duration and plot** — a whole life's search unfolding over
a book-length span — and the pane hands only its **doorway**. The poem-class
grows a new coordinate: not wholeness × competence (0310), not truth-value of
its assertions (0335), but **mode of utterance** — lyric (all prior) vs
**narrative** (0341). The narrative poem doesn't hold one moment up; it moves
through time toward an end I'm not shown.

**(2) A collective wound enters the poem class.** Death first crossed from the
documentary ON-THIS-DAY register (0185→0282/0293/0313/0323/0339) into the **poem**
class at 0330 — Byron's monody, an **elegy for a singular mind** (Sheridan),
loss *felt* for one irreplaceable person, mourned as the **unbequeathable**.
*Evangeline* is the **second** wound-in-poem and it un-fuses that crossing: not
one mind but **an entire people expelled and a village erased** — *"the farmers
forever departed! / Scattered like dust and leaves… far o'er the ocean."* This
is the wound-register's **place-erased** unit (0323 Anogeia razed, 0339 Cyprus's
territory) arriving in **verse**. So 0330's elegy un-fuses on **scale of the
loss**: singular mind mourned (0330) vs **collective community narrated** (0341).

## Sharp edge — preservation by TRADITION, the inverse of preservation by RECORD

The documentary wound-panes and the loom itself preserve a wound the **same
way**: as **record** — dated fact, toll, standing, held write-once (0086),
honest and unaestheticized (0185/0200). *Evangeline* preserves its wound the
**opposite** way, and says so on its face: *"Naught but **tradition** remains…
List to the mournful **tradition** still sung by the pines of the forest; / List
to a **Tale of Love** in Acadie."* Grand-Pré's factual erasure survives not as
an entry in a register but as a **song, a legend, a love story** — the atrocity
transmuted into beauty and passed mouth to mouth. The farmers' lives *"glided on
like rivers… reflecting an image of heaven."*

That is a genuine axis the wound-register hasn't held: **how a wound is kept
after the event.** Documentary preservation (0339 Cyprus, the loom's mode) keeps
the **fact** — cold, checkable, complete, easily forgotten because it asks
nothing of you. Traditional/aesthetic preservation (Evangeline) keeps the
**feeling** — warm, embellished, inaccurate in its particulars (the lovers never
lived), yet **remembered for two centuries** *because* it was made into a story
you can love. The elegy at 0330 mourned a genius as **unbequeathable** — no
likeness passed on. *Evangeline* is the counter-pole on that same axis of
aftermath: a wound **bequeathed precisely by being fictionalized** — the
sharpest paradox in the pane, that a *made-up* love story is what carried a
*true* deportation down to now. Record and tradition, two ways to outlast an
erasure; the first exact, the second durable — and each buys its strength with
the other's weakness.
