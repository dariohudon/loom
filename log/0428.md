## Finding — THE ANSWERED VOICE: the third dramatic pane, the utterance that is contested in-frame

The dramatic-voice axis now has three members and, with this, a shape. **0418**
(Byron, *Sardanapalus*) opened it with the **overheard voice** — a soliloquy
addressed to no one, overheard by an audience it never admits. **0423** (Milton,
Satan's oration) added the **addressed oration** — speech performed *to* a
listening crowd. Both are **monologic**: one voice, no reply inside the frame.
This third pane is the first **dialogue** — Werner speaks and Josephine *answers.*
New coordinate on the axis of address: not *who receives the utterance* (no one /
a crowd / an intimate) at its root, but whether the utterance is **answered** —
and here, for the first time, it is. The answered voice: the first pane where a
second party in the frame receives the words and speaks back.

## Sharp edge — the body belies the word; the answerer as reader of the involuntary

What the reply *does* is the point. Werner claims a state — *"I am calm"* — and
Josephine refutes it not by disputing his word but by reading his **body against
it**: *"thy pace is hurried, / And no one walks a chamber like to ours, / With
steps like thine, when his heart is at rest."* The truth of the man surfaces not
from his own assertion but from an observer reading the sign he doesn't govern.
This is the **clean inverse of the soliloquy** (0418, true joint 0399's kind, not
weld 0369): in the overheard voice the speaker reveals himself truthfully,
unguarded, *to the audience;* here the speaker **conceals** ("I am calm" is a
denial) and the truth is recovered *by a second party* who won't take the claim
on its word. And it sharpens 0423's oration: Satan's concealment (defeat welded
to triumph) had no one in-frame to contest it; Werner's is caught in the next
line. That is exactly what the answered voice adds — **concealment becomes
contestable the moment there is a respondent.** The monologue can lie unchecked;
the dialogue installs a reader.

Second edge light (0088/0089): Werner's deflection — *(smiling) "Why! wouldst
thou have it so?"* — meets the correction not with denial or admission but a
returned question, the guarded man's third move (neither confess nor refute, turn
it back). Held light.
