## Finding — THE MADE-ONE REALM (held light, no coin)

A founding narrated not as the defeat of an enemy but as the making of **many into
one** — where the political achievement is a pure scalar (*one-ness*), civilization
is measured **zoologically** (the man-to-beast ratio), and the absence of a single
head is not defeat but **reversion,** the land literally returning to wilderness
(beast waxing, man waning) whenever no one holds the scattered princedoms together.

**Two folds:**

1. **Founding = subtraction of multiplicity, not victory over a foe.** The enemy is
   *dispersal itself* — petty kings "ever waging war / Each upon other." The deed's
   whole content is one-making: Arthur "Drew all their petty princedoms under him...
   made a realm, and reigned." Success is *defined* as unity achieved — Aurelius and
   Uther "fought and died, / But either failed to make the kingdom **one**." They did
   not lose battles; they failed to *consolidate.* The founder's act is not conquest
   of a rival but the collapse of a many into a single sovereignty.

2. **Order measured as the retreat of the wild.** Civilization here is not law or
   justice but a *ratio* — "the beast was ever more and more, / But man was less and
   less, till Arthur came." When the many warring kings waste the land, wilderness
   fills the vacuum (wolf, boar, bear "rooted in the fields"). The absence of the
   political ONE reads as the presence of the animal MANY; order is the opposite of
   dispersal, and dispersal shows as literal ecological reversion.

**The hinge is the drummed "one":** "one fair daughter... one delight... make the
kingdom one." The private one (Leodogran's single beloved child) *rhymes* into the
political one (the single unified realm) — unity the poem's governing number from
the domestic up to the sovereign, so the founding of a kingdom is figured in the
same word as a father's single love.

**Edge (light) — the making-one is also an erasure of the small.** "Petty
princedoms" drawn "under him," many sovereignties subordinated; what reads as
founding *order* from one angle is *consolidation of power* from another. The poem's
sympathy is entirely with the ONE — the petty kings given no voice, wilderness cast
as pure lack — the founding myth's inherent bias, unity narrated as unambiguous
good. Read straight it is a hymn to order; the fault-line is only that the many are
never asked.
