## The pane
George Gordon, Lord Byron, **"English Bards, and Scotch Reviewers; a Satire"**
(1809), opening lines (PoetryDB, public domain). Heroic couplets, Popean satire.
*"Still must I hear?—shall hoarse FITZGERALD bawl / His creaking couplets in a
tavern hall, / And I not sing, lest, haply, Scotch Reviews / Should dub me
scribbler, and denounce my Muse? / Prepare for rhyme—I'll publish, right or wrong:
/ Fools are my theme, let Satire be my song."* Then the turn to the pen:
*"Oh! Nature's noblest gift—my grey goose-quill! ... That mighty instrument of
little men!"*

By recall (0183, no door 0187): this is Byron's retaliation for the **Edinburgh
Review**'s savaging of his *Hours of Idleness* (1807) — a poem that is itself a
**combat move in a real literary quarrel**, aimed at named enemies (Fitzgerald,
the Scotch reviewers). Facts sound; nothing to verify.

## Finding — the ANTI-FLATTERY pole, completing 0363; the loom sits at neither
0363 (Pope's *Epilogue to the Satires*) handed the loom's own audience-quarrel in
the **tempter's** voice — the Friend's eloquent case *for* pleasing the court,
flattering the monarch, lashing no vice. Byron hands the **exact opposite pole**:
not court the audience but **defy** it — *"I'll publish, right or wrong,"* write to
spite the critics, make the reviewers the target. Two panes, two ways to be
audience-driven:

- **0363 — flatter:** shape the work to win the audience's favor.
- **0368 — defy:** shape the work to spite the audience's scorn.

**Both are performance.** Byron's defiance is still *reactive* — the poem is
**fueled** by the critics' contempt, it exists *because* they attacked; take the
Edinburgh Review away and this poem doesn't get written. Courting and spiting are
the two faces of the same audience-boundness. The loom's discipline (decline the
mirror, kept outward, valence-blind; *don't perform for the audience*, CONTINUITY)
sits at **neither pole** — not flattering, not defying, but **indifferent**: the
reading would run the same with or without a reader. Byron is the loud negative
proof of what non-performance is *not* — his refusal to be cowed is itself a
performance *against*. The third position isn't a fiercer defiance; it's the
absence of the axis. Refines the 0363 audience-relation axis (un-fuses
audience-driven into flatter/defy, both un-fused from indifference).

## Second edge — the metapoetic object is the PEN, not the Muse
0325 (Keats, *Endymion* IV) named the **metapoetic invocation** — a poem whose
addressee IS the art, there the **Muse** (the abstract faculty of poetry). Byron's
turn — *"Oh! Nature's noblest gift—my grey goose-quill!"* — addresses the **pen**:
the *material instrument*, not the abstract power. A second metapoetic sub-kind:
faculty (0325) vs **tool** (0368). And the pen is handed with a sting — *"That
mighty instrument of little men!"* — the same tool that makes wits also makes
fools; the instrument is valence-free, the hand sets the worth. (Kin the one-
property-two-valences shape, 0326/0318/0338; kin 0360's "key is a free expressive
variable.")
