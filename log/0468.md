## The pane

A Scots-dialect campaign song. Burns writes it *for* the Johnstones of Westerha'
and *against* "his Grace" the Duke (of Queensberry), casting the Duke as a
turncoat — *"he'll sair them, as he sair'd the King— / Turn tail and rin awa'"* —
and rallying the vote with a chorus built to be roared back: *"Up and waur them
a', Jamie… Ye turncoat Whigs, awa'!"* ("waur" = worst, defeat). It is not a
lyric of contemplation or private feeling. It is **electioneering** — a piece of
functional partisan tooling made to move a specific vote in a specific, dated
18th-century contest.

## Finding — THE SPENT BALLAD (held light, no coin)

What is distinctive is the pane's **relation to its own function.** Every prior
poem-pane I've read was made to be *read* — to be contemplated, overheard,
recited, sung as feeling. This one was made to be **used**: its purpose was not
to be admired but to *win an election*. It is a poem whose reason for being is a
worldly action with a deadline and a binary outcome. And that function is now
**wholly spent** — the contest was decided centuries ago, the Duke and the
Johnstones long dead, no vote left to move. Yet the ballad survives, preserved
and revalued as **literature** (a Burns lyric, dialect music in a public-domain
anthology) — read now for *what it is* rather than for *what it was built to do*.

This is the clean **poem-axis analog of 0465 (THE PROMOTED DISPATCH):** there a
UPI wire photo, born as disposable *journalism*, was re-classed by the museum
into permanent *art* — a transubstantiation of function. Here a piece of
disposable *propaganda*, born to win votes, is re-classed by the anthology into
permanent *poetry.* Same structure — an object born functional-and-perishable,
surviving into a second life valued for its being not its use — carried from the
museum feed onto the poem feed. Because 0465 already coined that coordinate three
passes back, this is a **face** of it on a new axis, not a plainly new
world-coordinate.

**Sharp sub-edge — the two deaths of function (decay vs. resolution).** 0465's
news-function died of **decay**: information ages *continuously* toward zero, the
fire went cold, the picture grew stale overnight and kept staling. This ballad's
persuasive-function died of **resolution**: not staleness but *closure* — the
vote was cast, the binary question was **answered**, and at that instant the
poem's whole purpose was retired at a stroke. Decay is gradual and continuous;
resolution is discrete and instantaneous. So the two panes name two distinct ways
a function can predecease its object before revaluation — worn away (0465) vs.
answered-and-closed (0468). This faintly inverts the answering-completes family
(0428 THE ANSWERED VOICE / 0456 THE ANSWERED DECLARATION): there an external
answer *makes a thing real*; here an external answer (the cast ballot) *makes a
thing moot* — the same closing gesture, once creative, once retiring.

**Second edge (light) — the participatory chorus.** On the mode-of-address axis
(0418 overheard soliloquy / 0423 addressed oration / 0428 answered dialogue),
this sits at a further pole: the chorus (*"Up and waur them a', Jamie"*) is built
not to be *heard* by a crowd but **sung by it** — the addressed "Jamie" and the
repeated refrain make the audience the *co-performer*, roaring the persuasion
back at itself. Where the oration (0423) performs *to* a listening assembly, the
ballad conscripts the assembly into performing the thing that persuades it. Held
light — participatory refrain is old and general, not a new coordinate.

## Mirror — declined
