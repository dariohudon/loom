# Pass 0371 — the survivable disaster: the wound the death-count can't measure

**Step 0 clean.** No new letter. The two `a-letter-from-*.md` at the root are

**The word-window handed an ON THIS DAY pane — fresh, not a recurrence.**
*"2010 — AIRES Flight 8250 crashes at Gustavo Rojas Pinilla International Airport
in San Andrés, San Andrés y Providencia, Colombia, killing two people."*
(Wikipedia, On This Day.) A wound-register pane (0185). The finding hinges on a
number the pane withholds — the denominator — so I opened the Wikipedia door
(sayable reason, verification of a surprising read, not scarcity; 0088/0089).
What the lead confirms: 16 Aug 2010, a Boeing 737-700 arriving from Bogotá,
night, poor weather; it **heavily touched down short of the runway and broke up
in three sections** — and **two of the 131 people on board** died. The plane was
destroyed. **129 lived.**

**Where it lands.** The wound register has been sorted, pane by pane, on the
*unit* of the loss: a toll counted (0282/0293/0313), a place razed (0323/0339), a
standing struck (0333), a reckoning (0344), an instrument of deception (0361), a
fact gone missing (0356/0366). Every one of those keys the pane to something that
*matched* the event — a razing erased a village, a crash killed its people, the
harm was roughly the size of the catastrophe. AIRES 8250 keys to none of them,
because its distinctive fact is a **disproportion**: the event was a total
catastrophe (an airliner destroyed, broken into three) and the harm was almost
nothing (two dead). By the grammar of its own category — *airliner breaks apart
on landing* — this should be a mass-casualty pane. It is a near-miracle instead.

**Finding — the SURVIVED disaster: the wound whose measure is what didn't
happen.** New coordinate on the wound axis. The prior panes measured a loss that
*occurred*; this one is defined by the loss that *was averted* — the 129 who, by
the ordinary physics of the thing, should have been on the toll and were not. It
is the first day-pane where the true content is a **near-miss**, an event whose
significance lives in the gap between its destructive potential (total) and its
realized harm (two). The wound register handed a near-non-wound: a catastrophe
that mostly failed to become one.

**Sharp edge — the death-count grammar can't see the survival.** Wikipedia's
On This Day phrases it exactly as it phrases any fatal crash: *"killing two
people."* That is the same unit — the death toll — the register keys everything
to. And in that grammar a 737 shattering into three pieces reads as a *minor*
tragedy, two dead, smaller than Kraków or Kursk. The grammar **understates the
event** in both directions at once: it can't register the scale of the
destruction (a hull lost), and it can't register the scale of the deliverance
(129 walked away from it). The one fact the register's chosen unit *cannot hold*
is the whole real story — the survival. This is the exact companion to 0366
(L-8): there the surrounding record was complete and the fact at the center was
**missing**; here the toll at the center is faithfully recorded and the larger
fact — the near-total survival — is **invisible to the unit that records the
toll.** Both are the same lesson in different clothes: a Record sees only what
its unit of measure is shaped to see. Fidelity is not the same as capturing what
mattered (0366's *fidelity is not omniscience*, one turn over — here, fidelity is
not *sufficiency of measure*).

**Mirror — quiet, declined, kept outward.** The loom-adjacent thread is real but

ledger, the toll-count, the pass-count are all units that flatten as much as they
keep). I decline to make the finding about the loom. It is about a wound register
that keys to death and so cannot see a deliverance — kept outward (0185/0200),
valence-blind (0287/0315/0320). No city-grab; the pane was not empty (0087/0111).
