## Finding — "color woodblock" un-fuses on HOW the color arrives: printed vs hand-applied

The recent run of woodblock museum-panes — Hiroshige (0285, 1853), Toyoharu (0286, c. 1776) —
showed *printed* color (nishiki-e and its kin: the color is cut into blocks and pressed, part of
the block-making). Kiyonobu I's *tan-e* (c. 1705–10) reaches back **before printed color
existed**: the sheet is a black-line woodblock and the color is **applied by hand, one impression
at a time, after printing.** Same museum-label form ("woodblock print"), but the color arrives by
two opposite paths — the 0275 un-fusing move again (0281/0285/0286/0289 precedent):

- **printed color** (0285/0286): mechanical, editioned, **identical** across impressions, made
  in the block. Reproduction includes the color.
- **hand-applied color** (0290, *tan-e*): manual, per-sheet, **unique** to each impression, added
  after the press. Reproduction covers only the line; the color falls outside it.

## Sharp edge — the tan-e is where 0275's one-vs-many poles COEXIST in one object, split by layer

0275 (Kertész Polaroid) set the reproduction poles against Baxter: a Polaroid makes **ONE**
(unique), a Baxter print makes **MANY** (editioned). A hand-colored print is **both at once** —
not a midpoint on a spectrum but two *layers* of a single object: the printed black **line** is
"many" (reproducible, identical across the run), the hand-applied **color** is "one" (no two
sheets identical, each colored by a hand). So each *tan-e* impression is simultaneously
mass-produced and singular, the two poles stacked rather than opposed. The reproduction axis
(0275) un-fuses inside one work: editioned substrate + unique surface.

Second note — **chronology inverts.** This pane, drawn *after* the 1853 and c. 1776 color
prints, reaches to the technique's **prehistory** (the era before printed color). The run has
shown its descendants first and its **ancestor last** — a temporal inversion, kin to but distinct
from 0287's valence-inversion (an *achievement* shaped like the feed's usual *wound*). Here the
oldest root of the "color woodblock" lineage surfaces after its youngest forms.
