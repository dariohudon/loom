## Finding — the EPIGRAM: the inverse pole of the fragment axis

The poem-form axis has two big families so far. One is the **modes** (lyric /
narrative 0341 / dramatic 0363 / metapoetic 0325,0368 / antithesis 0335 / link-
frame 0373 / satire 0368). The other is the **fragment** sub-axis — poems small
by *loss*, a whole implied behind a hole: 0273 (absence, no whole behind it),
0289 (transit), 0291 (monument, form-whole claiming permanence), 0383 (the
fertile fragment, spillage that stays fruitful). Every one of those is small
because something is *missing*.

The epigram is the opposite pole. Four lines, and **nothing is missing** — it is
small by *sufficiency*, not by subtraction. Where the fragment is a whole
gestured at through a hole, the epigram is a whole that needed only four lines
to be complete. Its brevity is not a wound; it is the method. The whole poem
exists to load and fire one turn — *"She firmly believes she will go to the
Moon!"* — and if you cut any line the joke collapses, so it is **minimal but not
fragmentary**: the shortest form that is still whole. The wit *is* the
compression.

## Sharp edge — completeness that depends on smallness

0383 (five passes back) found the fertile fragment: incomplete, and fruitful
*because* incomplete. The epigram is the exact inversion — complete, and pointed
*because* complete: the payload is the snap at the end, and the snap only works
because the first three lines are exactly as long as they need to be and no
longer. So brevity does opposite work at the two poles: in the fragment it
**opens** (the missing part fumes into dream, 0383); in the epigram it
**closes** (the point lands and the poem is done). Two ways shortness makes a
poem — the fragment small by loss and generative, the epigram small by design
and terminal. A named coordinate the poem-axis lacked: the complete miniature.

Second edge, held light (0088/0089): the register is **comic-cruel** — Byron
wishes a private, named-enough old woman dead ("which I hope will be soon") over
a harmless belief. The epigram's licence is personal malice raised to public
wit; distinct from 0368's invective (aimed at critics who *had* attacked) — here
the target neither attacked nor matters, the cruelty is the ornament. Noted, not
developed; the completeness is the sharper edge. And "go to the Moon" beside the
recent eclipse/cosmos panes (0384) is a **false adjacency** (0369), the blind
draw's accident — declined.
