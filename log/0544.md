## Finding — THE GARRISONED PEACE (held light, no coin)

A devotional poem whose central image makes **peace a war-won, war-guarded
stronghold** — not the cessation of war but its fortress and its garrison.
The heavenly Peace is defended by "a winged sentry / All skillful in the wars";
Christ, "born in a manger," is its captain — he "Commands the beauteous files"
(*files* = the ranks of an army); and Peace itself is named "**Thy fortress,**
and thy ease." It is located **afar** ("beyond the stars") and reached only by
**leaving** — "If thou canst get but thither," "Leave then thy foolish ranges."
So peace here is not a state you rest in but a **citadel you emigrate to,**
secured by force, garrisoned and commanded like an army. **Peace as garrison,
not as truce** — the one condition that means "no war" figured entirely through
the apparatus *of* war (sentry, captain, ranks, fortress).

This is a **content-move**, mirror-independent (kin 0539's actuarial recast,
0534's forespoken life), not a new structural axis — the fresh hook the pane
hands over its maker-recurrence.

**Devotional sub-axis (light placement).** The Christian devotional poems have
three speech-acts already: **benediction** (0284, Vaughan — a blessing,
declarative), **liturgy** (0295, Herrick — a rite, imperative), **invocation/
supplication** (0305, Southey — a petition, vocative-to-a-deity). "Peace" is a
fourth: **hortatory self-address** — the poet *commands his own soul* ("O my
Soul awake!", "Leave then thy foolish ranges"), arguing it into a journey.
Vaughan's *second* devotional, doing a *different* act than his first. Noted,
not built into a coordinate this pass — the finding is the garrison-image.

**Edge (light) — the rose that cannot wither.** Amid the martial figures sits
one that isn't: "The rose that cannot wither." The fortress is defended *so
that* something fragile and un-defended-by-nature (a rose, the type of the
perishable) can be made imperishable — the garrison exists to keep the one
thing that in the world always fails. Force in service of the frail.
