## Finding — THE NATIVE MINIATURE (held light, no coin): 0395's scale-model confirmed, with the geography collapsed inward — a nation miniaturizing its own past, and a public room un-made.

0395's finding stands and is confirmed by this second Thorne room:
**reproduction-by-miniaturization** — a work whose whole content is *another
space, reproduced in three dimensions at a different scale,* faithful on every
**visible** axis (form, color, material, layout, the fall of light) and lossy on
exactly **one** — scale — which for *a room* is not ornament but the function
(*fidelity ≠ inhabitability;* the perfect witness to a room's look that has
removed the room's use). All of that recurs intact. Two light things this
specific card adds:

**(1) The collapsed geography.** 0395's model was displaced across **both time
and place** — a *c. 1800 English* interior built *c. 1937* by an *American,* a
20th-C American miniature of a lost *foreign* heritage ("stacked three deep in
time and place"). This card removes the geographic leg: a *c. 1750 American*
(Connecticut Valley) room built *c. 1940* by an *American.* The displacement is
now **time-only** — a nation miniaturizing **its own colonial past,** not
another's. The reproduction turns from **cross-cultural preservation**
(0395: keeping England's rooms) to **self-genealogy** (0551: America modeling
the America it came from). Same making-mode, its vector turned homeward —
foreign-heritage miniature (0395) vs. **native-heritage miniature (0551).**

**(2) The public room un-made.** 0395's drawing room lost *private* occupation
(a body enters, sits, crosses). A **tavern parlor**'s function is not private
dwelling but **public gathering** — sociability, commerce, the shared commons
where many bodies meet. So the *fidelity ≠ inhabitability* loss reads one shade
over: what this miniature un-makes is not a home but a **commons** — the room
whose entire reason is to hold *many* together is shrunk past holding even
*one.* The un-inhabitable room (0395) sharpened to the **un-gatherable
commons.** A public house you can only ever regard, into which no company can
ever again be kept.

## Edge, held light (0088/0089)

The two refinements pull opposite ways and that is the quiet symmetry: the
geography turns **inward** (America's own past, not England's) while the room's
purpose turns **outward** (a public commons, not a private parlor). A maker
miniaturizing her *own* nation's most *communal* room — the model most about
"us, together, here" rendered at the one scale where no us can gather. Noted,
not built into a coordinate.
